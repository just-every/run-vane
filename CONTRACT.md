# Vane contract

Target edition: 17 October 2026. Vane is complete privacy-focused website analytics for a US small or midsize business that wants to replace Plausible Analytics, Fathom Analytics or Matomo Cloud, with the depth buyers now expect from Umami, Simple Analytics and PostHog Web Analytics. It counts cookie-free unique visitors, visits, bounce rate and visit duration. It shows real-time and historical traffic by page, source, campaign, location and device. It measures goals, funnels and revenue, and it reconciles them with real orders and leads. It sends scheduled reports and alerts and follows them up, and none of this needs a cookie banner. Agents keep the measurement honest: they explain why numbers moved, propose and ship changes under authority the owner grants, and measure the result.

This contract is the product Vane is building towards, not a description of today's code. The [Status](#status) section says, clause by clause, how close the code at the judged commit is. A gap in the code never narrows a clause. Every clause names the behavior a buyer gets and the acceptance evidence, a journey or test through a public door, that proves it. ACCEPTANCE.md lists the same evidence for each build round. This documentation round changes no homepage, interface or runtime behavior.

## Product target

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Category | Privacy-focused website analytics |
| Incumbents a buyer switches from | Plausible Analytics, Fathom Analytics, Matomo Cloud (and the up-and-comers Umami, Simple Analytics and PostHog Web Analytics) |
| First buyer | A US small or midsize business owner or marketing lead who runs their own websites (a services firm, a Shopify or WooCommerce store, an agency managing client sites), paying in USD and running Vane in their own Cloudflare account |

What switching means: the buyer installs one script, or a WordPress, Shopify, Webflow, Squarespace, Wix, Google Tag Manager or Pane path. Their Google Analytics 4, Plausible, Fathom, Matomo, Umami or Simple Analytics history comes across, their existing `plausible()` and `fathom.trackEvent()` calls keep counting, their goals and custom properties are recreated for review from the sources their plan exposes, and their funnels are rebuilt in a guided step. On the first day they see the same visitors, visits, bounce rate, duration, sources, campaigns, locations, devices, goals, funnels and revenue the incumbent showed, under published definitions. Reports, alerts, shared dashboards, team access, the Stats API, Search Console keywords and a first-party proxy all work. No cookie banner is required for the default mode.

A buyer replacing an incumbent needs the whole job, so the target is the whole job:

- **Install and collect.** One script with live verification, platform install paths, SPA support, a server-side Events API, a first-party proxy, open path collection with redaction, and bot, spam and internal-traffic exclusion at scale (`INSTALL-*`, `COLLECT-*`, `COVERAGE-001`).
- **Measure people and engagement without cookies.** Daily-rotating salted hashes for unique visitors and visits, bounce, duration, scroll depth and a disclosed methodology. A consented mode adds returning visitors and retention (`METRIC-*`).
- **Understand traffic.** Real-time and historical dashboards; page, entry and exit, channel, referrer, UTM, hostname, location and device breakdowns; filters, segments, comparisons, annotations, journeys, site search, page performance and aggregate heatmaps (`DASH-*`, `PERF-001`, `HEAT-001`).
- **Measure conversions and revenue.** Goals, custom events with properties, automatic outbound-link, download, 404 and form tracking, funnels, revenue and ecommerce, conversion rates by source, experiments, and reconciliation with Stripe, Shopify, WooCommerce, Stall, HubSpot, Orbit, Pencil and Dial outcomes (`CONV-*`, `EXPERIMENT-001`).
- **Connect search and advertising.** Google Search Console and Bing Webmaster keywords, and ad spend from Google Ads, Microsoft Ads, Meta and LinkedIn matched to campaigns (`SEARCH-*`, `ADS-001`).
- **Move in.** GA4, Plausible, Fathom, Matomo, Umami, Simple Analytics and PostHog history, merged without double counting. On cut-over day the incumbent's tracking calls keep counting, its goals and properties are recovered from what its plan exposes, and its funnels are rebuilt in a guided step (`IMPORT-*`).
- **Report and follow up.** Scheduled email, Slack, Teams and Nook reports; spike, drop, conversion and tracking-health alerts; and follow-through that stays open until a person resolves it (`REPORT-*`, `ALERT-*`, `FOLLOW-001`).
- **Share and administer.** Shared, embedded and public dashboards; per-site roles, SSO and agency client workspaces; the Stats API, CSV and warehouse export, webhooks and MCP; privacy disclosure, consent managers and US state privacy signals; owner-run hosting with measured cost; and a phone-ready dashboard and native apps (`SHARE-*`, `TEAM-*`, `AGENCY-001`, `API-*`, `PRIVACY-*`, `HOST-001`, `MOBILE-*`).
- **Push further.** Agents keep the instrumentation correct and explain changes. They propose and ship site or campaign changes under granted authority, and they measure what happened against coverage gaps (`AGENT-*`).

### Neighboring products stay separate

Vane measures traffic and outcomes. It links to the products that own the neighboring jobs instead of rebuilding them:

- [Pane](https://github.com/just-every/run-pane) builds the website. Vane gives it a one-switch install path (`INSTALL-004`) and, under an owner's grant, drafts page changes there (`AGENT-003`).
- [Stall](https://github.com/just-every/run-stall) runs the storefront; Shopify, WooCommerce and Stripe are the external equivalents. Their orders confirm revenue conversions (`CONV-007`, `CONV-008`).
- [Orbit](https://github.com/just-every/run-orbit) (or HubSpot) is the CRM, [Pencil](https://github.com/just-every/run-pencil) the form builder and [Dial](https://github.com/just-every/run-dial) the booking tool. They confirm leads and bookings (`CONV-008`).
- [Mango](https://github.com/just-every/run-mango) sends email campaigns. Its sends become annotations and its links carry UTM tags (`DASH-009`).
- [Uptime](https://github.com/just-every/run-uptime) monitors availability and [Spool](https://github.com/just-every/run-spool) tracks errors. Their incidents become annotations and evidence in explanations (`DASH-009`, `AGENT-002`).
- [Crank](https://github.com/just-every/run-crank) (or Zapier and Make) automates downstream work from Vane webhooks (`API-003`), [Nook](https://github.com/just-every/run-nook) carries reports and alerts like Slack (`REPORT-002`), and [Auth](https://github.com/just-every/run-auth) can be the sign-in provider (`TEAM-002`).
- [Penny](https://github.com/just-every/run-penny) owns product feedback and roadmaps. Vane does not collect feedback.

## Existing guarantees

These clauses restate what the released first contract and ACCEPTANCE.md promised. They stay in force; where the target widens them, the target clause names which one it extends.

- **HTTP-001 — One Worker with honest public and private doors.** One Cloudflare Worker serves the installed instance. The Lobby is at `/`, with `/health`, `/signin` and `/setup`. The collection script is at `/vane.js`, with its collection and configuration endpoints. Private `/app` and `/api/vane/*` routes are behind native sessions, and unknown paths return real 404 responses. The public run site is a separate static surface. **Acceptance evidence:** Request `/health`, `/vane.js`, an unknown path and every private route signed out on a built Worker. Health answers, the script is served, unknown paths return 404, and private routes return 401 or redirect to sign-in (`tests/vane.test.mjs`).

- **ACCESS-001 — Owners approve, administrators prepare, members read.** Roles come from the native operator accounts. Members read sites and reports; `TEAM-001` makes each member a Viewer, who reads and adds notes, or an Analyst, who also prepares goal, funnel and no-code event changes; no member prepares a site, other settings or erasure. Administrators also prepare site, settings and erasure proposals. Only the owner approves proposals, imports, restores and exports history. A revoked operator loses access on their next request. **Acceptance evidence:** Invite an administrator and a member. The member cannot prepare a site, the administrator cannot approve or import, export is owner-only, and revoking the administrator ends their session (`tests/vane.test.mjs`).

- **ACCESS-002 — Private doors refuse signed-out and cross-origin requests.** Every private read and mutation needs a native session, and every mutation needs the configured same origin. A direct transport call gets the same refusal as a browser route. **Acceptance evidence:** Call every private GET and POST door signed out, signed out after sign-out, and with a foreign Origin. Each is refused with no write (`tests/vane.test.mjs`).

- **APPROVE-001 — Prepared changes apply only on the owner's recorded approval.** A site, a settings change or a history erasure can be prepared in the UI or through the agent command API. Each change waits as a proposal until the owner approves it once through the approval form. The approver, the preparer and the time are recorded. Approval refuses a proposal whose site version has changed. The command API can prepare and read proposals but can never approve them. The one exception is the owner's provisioning key (`API-001`): the owner's standing approval, under which each site, goal or shared link it asks for is prepared and approved at once in the owner's name and audited via `key:<label>`. **Acceptance evidence:** Prepare as an administrator, approve as the owner, then approve again; the second approval gets 409. Inspect the proposal for distinct preparer and approver (`tests/vane.test.mjs`).

- **COLLECT-001 — A cookie-free script that sends only declared anonymous fields.** `vane.js` sets no cookie or browser storage except the two declared exceptions below. In default mode it sends no visitor or session identifier, user agent, raw URL or query values. Only the consented-mode exception (`METRIC-005`) adds a transient random identifier and its issue time after explicit consent; only its site-scoped digest is stored. When Do Not Track or Global Privacy Control is set, Vane does nothing, with one exception: when the owner enables the `PRIVACY-002` counter, that page load sends one field-free count and nothing else. Collection accepts events only from the site's registered origin and only with the declared field set. It refuses extra fields, such as an email address. This clause constrains the field set: no stored raw identifier, ever. The hostname field belongs to `COLLECT-002`/`DASH-013`, and the target field set and server-side derivations are widened by `COLLECT-002`, `DASH-013`, `METRIC-001`, `DASH-005`/`DASH-006`, `PERF-001` (page timings), `CONV-002` (custom events and properties), `CONV-006` (revenue amounts) and `CONV-007` (ecommerce product lines) and `METRIC-005` (consented random identifier and issue time, raw values discarded before storage) under that constraint. The target allows exactly two declared exceptions to "no browser storage", and the methodology page (`METRIC-004`) and data inventory (`PRIVACY-001`) name both. One is the owner's own-browser exclusion (`COLLECT-003`): a single `vane_ignore` localStorage flag holding no identifier, written only when the owner opts that browser out with `#vane-ignore=on` and cleared with `#vane-ignore=off`. The other is the consented identifier (`METRIC-005`), written only after granted consent. A visitor in the default mode gets no cookie and no storage. `COLLECT-001` is complete with the `vane_ignore` own-browser exclusion, both storage exceptions named on `/methodology` and in the report's data inventory, and its browser proof; adding the hostname is `COLLECT-002`/`DASH-013` work. The public script-config door returns only the script’s consent/adapter, declined counter, sampling, automatic-event, kept-query, search-parameter and approved event-definition settings. Only when the owner has approved incumbent compatibility (`IMPORT-008`) does it add `incumbentCompatibility: true` and the site’s ISO currency; compatibility off is byte-for-byte the earlier response. Only when the owner enables `METRIC-005` does the config additionally expose `consentedVisitors: true` and its approved `consentLifetimeMonths`; the mode being off adds neither field. Excluded IPs/countries, goals, funnels, source rules, retention, public-report settings and secrets never leave through this door. **Acceptance evidence:** Post an event from a foreign origin (403), with an extra personal field (400) and with a path carrying a query (400). A valid event is accepted (202) (`tests/vane.test.mjs`). In a browser, a default-mode pageview leaves no cookie and no storage entry; `#vane-ignore=on` writes only `vane_ignore` and excludes that browser, and `#vane-ignore=off` clears it. `/methodology` and the report's data inventory name both storage exceptions. With DNT and then GPC set, a page load sends nothing while the `PRIVACY-002` counter is off, and exactly one field-free count and nothing else while it is on. The public-config journey asserts both compatibility modes: an unapproved proposal changes nothing; approval adds only the compatibility flag and ISO currency; private fields stay absent, and approved opt-out restores the exact earlier response bytes.

- **CONSENT-001 — The site's consent mode is enforced at collection.** A site chooses whether consent is required. When it is, only events that carry granted consent are admitted. Consent-mode changes go through `APPROVE-001`. **Acceptance evidence:** On a consent-required site, an event without granted consent is refused with 403 and one with granted consent is accepted (`tests/vane.test.mjs`).

- **DEDUP-001 — Each page load is admitted once.** A fresh UUID identifies each page load. A repeat with identical data is acknowledged as a duplicate without counting twice, and a repeat with different data is refused. **Acceptance evidence:** Send the same event twice (202, then 200 with nothing counted) and then a conflicting copy (409). The report counts one visit (`tests/vane.test.mjs`).

- **TRAFFIC-001 — Page loads, sources, campaigns and goals with a comparison and stated limits.** For any date range (default: the last seven days), the report shows accepted page loads, goal conversions, referring hostnames, campaigns, pages and goals. The totals and every breakdown row (referring hostnames, campaigns, pages and goals) carry their previous-period count and change, compared with the preceding period of the same length. Beside them, the report states the limits of collection coverage: sampling, consent, configured paths, imports and unknown coverage. The page-load count stays visible as pageviews when `METRIC-001` and `METRIC-002` add visitors and visits. **Acceptance evidence:** Collect events in two adjacent periods of the same length and read the summary. The compared totals and every referring-hostname, campaign, page and goal row show the correct previous-period count and change, and the counts and coverage-limit text match the seeded events.

- **IMPORT-001 — Reviewed, journaled and undoable Umami import.** The owner previews a documented Umami v2 Events page without any write. Visitor identifiers, titles and raw URLs are discarded before custody. The owner then approves the exact reviewed document by its fingerprint. The import is idempotent and resumable, every effect is journaled, and an undo removes its contributions while the journal remains. **Acceptance evidence:** Preview the import (no writes, no raw identifiers), apply it with a changed fingerprint (409), apply it twice (one job), read the journal and undo it (`tests/vane.test.mjs`).

- **ARCHIVE-001 — Complete history export and atomic restore.** The owner exports every analytics, proposal, deletion and import-custody table as an integrity-checked archive, including each site's US metro-name table with its source label and public choice (`DASH-005`). Restore works only into an empty installation, keeps the owner sign-in, rejects a corrupt archive and preserves import undo. **Acceptance evidence:** Export, corrupt the archive and restore it (400, no writes). Restore the real archive into an empty installation, check that the tables are identical, restore it again (409) and undo the restored import (`tests/vane.test.mjs`).

- **ERASURE-001 — Owner-approved erasure, fenced against old backups.** An approved history erasure removes a site's live and imported events and retires its import custody. It records a deletion journal, and a later journal refuses the restore of an older archive that still holds the erased history. **Acceptance evidence:** Erase a site with live and imported history. Both are gone, the deletion and erasure journals exist, and restoring the old archive with the later journal is refused (`tests/vane.test.mjs`).

- **RETENTION-001 — The declared retention window erases older history.** Each site declares 30, 90 or 365 days of retention (the `PRIVACY-001` target adds 13 and 25 months and unlimited). Applying retention erases older live and imported events in resumable steps and records the deletion in the journal. **Acceptance evidence:** Import old history, collect a recent event and apply retention until it completes. The old history is gone, the recent event remains, and one deletion is journaled (`tests/vane.test.mjs`).

- **RUNSITE-001 — The public run site reaches installation and every guide.** The public homepage links to `/run` and `/docs`. `/run` shows the real `rer dev just-every/run-vane` launch command, and every guide's local links resolve. The run site contains only public routes, reads no installation data and loads only local assets; Run and Docs work without JavaScript. **Acceptance evidence:** `rer build --site`, then follow every local link from the built homepage and guides (`tests/site.test.mjs`).

- **DOCS-001 — Documentation built from repository Markdown.** `/docs` renders the repository's Markdown guides with readable code blocks, tables, section links and optional copy-to-clipboard controls. **Acceptance evidence:** Build the site and open each guide. Code blocks, tables and heading anchors render, and the clipboard control copies the block.

- **HOME-001 — The approved homepage in the shared theme.** The run site serves the owner-approved design-desk landing page (17 Oct 2026: the weather vane in an open sky, the swallow mark, Inter Tight and Inter type; page-s1: one launch week at Juniper Coffee, dated section by section, with the hero's wind carried down the page as one trail) in the shared theme with locally served fonts and artwork. At phone width its hero cards and every section's drawing stack under their copy. Its Juniper Coffee figures are explanatory drawings and are never seeded as analytics. **Acceptance evidence:** The independent visual gate compares the built homepage at 1440 and 390 px with the approved specification and picks. In the repository, a browser opens the built homepage at 390 and 1440 px: at 390 px the hero cards and every section's drawing sit under their copy with no sideways scrolling, at 1440 px each two-column section's drawing sits beside its copy and the wind trail runs down the page, and every font, image and style comes from the run site itself, with Inter Tight and Inter loaded.

- **LOBBY-001 — The installed Lobby and real sign-in stay intact.** An installed instance keeps its Lobby at `/`, real sign-in and first-run setup flows, and its existing authenticated routes, all in the shared Vane theme. **Acceptance evidence:** On a fresh installation, bootstrap the owner, complete `/setup`, sign in by email link and open `/app`. No landing-drawing numbers appear in analytics.

- **DEPLOY-001 — Run-site deployment stays behind its switch.** Run-site deployment runs only when the owner sets `RUN_SITE_DEPLOY`. The run site is configured for run-vane.com. Existing edition, package, Worker and database identifiers are preserved. **Acceptance evidence:** With the variable unset, the run-site workflow skips deployment. The identifiers in `wrangler.jsonc`, `package.json` and `edition.json` are unchanged.

## Target clauses

### Install and collection

- **INSTALL-001 — One script tag with live install verification.** Settings show the snippet with a copy button. A **Verify installation** action loads the site's homepage server-side: the Worker sends a GET for the registered origin's public homepage, following up to five redirects, carries no visitor data and stores only the pass/fail result. The owner's own website is not an integration, so the check has no `seed.json` externals entry, and it is listed under Declared data flows (`PRIVACY-005`). The action finds the script and waits up to 60 seconds for a first event from that page. It then reports the exact cause of any failure: script missing, wrong site ID, origin mismatch, Content Security Policy blocking, consent flag never set, path excluded, or a blocker suspected. Until a first event arrives, the site shows **Awaiting first event** in the overview. **Acceptance evidence:** Point a fixture site at Vane without the script, then with the wrong site ID, then with a CSP that blocks it, then correctly. Each check names its cause; the correct one turns verified when the first event arrives. Record the Worker's outbound requests during each check: only the homepage GET and at most five redirects, carrying no visitor data, and only the pass/fail result is stored.

- **INSTALL-002 — WordPress and WooCommerce plugin.** A WordPress.org plugin connects a WordPress site to a Vane installation with a site token. It inserts the script (or the proxied script from `INSTALL-006`), excludes logged-in administrators if the owner chooses, and turns on outbound-link, download, 404 and form events (`CONV-003`). With WooCommerce it records product views, add to cart, checkout and purchases with revenue (`CONV-007`). It shows a Vane dashboard widget in wp-admin. **Acceptance evidence:** Install the plugin on a WordPress + WooCommerce test site, connect it and place a test order. Pageviews, automatic events and a purchase with the correct USD revenue appear in Vane, the `CONV-007` ecommerce funnel and product report show the order's product, quantity and revenue, and admin visits are excluded when that option is set.

- **INSTALL-003 — Shopify app with checkout tracking.** A Shopify app installs the script on the storefront through a theme app extension. It registers a Customer Events web pixel so that checkout steps and purchases (order value, currency, product lines) arrive as events with no customer personal data. Orders confirmed through the Shopify Admin API reconcile the purchases (`CONV-008`). **Acceptance evidence:** Install the app on a Shopify development store and complete a test checkout. Storefront pageviews, the checkout funnel steps and a reconciled purchase with its revenue appear, and the `CONV-007` ecommerce funnel and product report show the order's product, quantity and revenue.

- **INSTALL-004 — Webflow, Squarespace, Wix, Pane and static-site paths.** Step-by-step install guides with a verification check (`INSTALL-001`) cover Webflow (site-wide custom code), Squarespace (code injection), Wix (custom code or Velo) and common static hosts (Netlify, Vercel, GitHub Pages). Each guide also shows how to send the platform's form submissions as events. A [Pane](https://github.com/just-every/run-pane) site turns Vane on with one switch, and Pane's editor shows that page's visitors. Pane can also apply a split-test variant (`EXPERIMENT-001`) to the page without code. A [Stall](https://github.com/just-every/run-stall) storefront turns Vane on the same way and emits the `CONV-007` ecommerce events (product view, add to cart, begin checkout and purchase with its product lines). **Acceptance evidence:** Follow each guide against its platform's test site and pass verification. Turn on Vane in a Pane site and see the page's visitors in the Pane editor. Turn on Vane in a Stall storefront and run a test purchase: the `CONV-007` ecommerce funnel and product report show the product, quantity and revenue. Apply a split-test variant (`EXPERIMENT-001`) from the Pane editor and see its exposures counted.

- **INSTALL-005 — Google Tag Manager template.** A published GTM Community Template installs Vane, passes GTM consent state into `PRIVACY-003`, and maps dataLayer events to Vane custom events with declared properties. **Acceptance evidence:** Import the template into a GTM container, publish it and fire a dataLayer purchase. A Vane event with the mapped properties arrives, and denied consent sends nothing in a consent-required mode.

- **INSTALL-006 — First-party proxy for blocker-resilient delivery.** The owner can serve the script and collection endpoint from their own domain. Ready-made setups cover a Cloudflare Worker route (one click when the site's zone is in the same Cloudflare account), Next.js rewrites, Vercel, Netlify, Nginx, Apache and WordPress (`INSTALL-002`). A custom collection subdomain such as `stats.example.com` is also supported. The proxied path forwards the visitor's country and user agent for server-side derivation and never logs them. Reports label how much traffic arrived through the proxy (`COVERAGE-001`). The implemented connection is created, rotated and revoked only by the active owner, with an audit record; external server credentials are shown once and stored only as a digest. The one-click action uses the owner’s transient Cloudflare token, refuses existing route or DNS collisions, and never retains that token. Registered script and configuration GETs remain public; collection still requires the registered website Origin and the ordinary consent and privacy rules. Proxy and direct shares count admitted native pageviews over their native transport denominator; historical rows without transport and imported coverage remain explicitly unknown. Every tracker door added later inherits this proxy boundary before it ships, including WordPress insertion under `INSTALL-002`. **Acceptance evidence:** Configure the Cloudflare route and the Next.js rewrite, and load the site in Chromium with uBlock Origin's default lists active, including EasyPrivacy. First prove the lists are live: in the same session, before the site loads, the incumbents' real tags `https://plausible.io/js/script.js` and `https://www.googletagmanager.com/gtag/js?id=G-TEST` fail with `net::ERR_BLOCKED_BY_CLIENT`. Record the unproxied `vane.js` outcome with the uBlock and list versions; when it is blocked the proxied page still collects. The journey reruns weekly against current default lists. No Vane surface claims to evade blockers or recover blocked visitors; COVERAGE-001's proxy/direct shares are the only measure. Events still arrive, the proxy share is reported, and the forwarded values are never stored raw.

- **INSTALL-007 — Single-page applications and hash routing.** The script records a pageview on every History API navigation and, when the owner turns it on, on hash changes. It also sends pageviews manually via `vane('pageview', {url, props})` for frameworks that control routing, and a manual or automatic pageview carries custom properties like any event (`CONV-002`). The exact rules: a pageview is sent on the document load and on every `history.pushState` and back or forward navigation; the owner turns on hash routing by adding `data-hash` to the snippet, and then every hash route change is a pageview too, with `#/about` (or `#!/about`) on `/app/` recorded as the path `/app/about`, the route's own query dropped, and a fragment that is not a route (such as `#pricing`) neither a navigation nor part of the path. With `data-manual` on the snippet the script sends no pageview by itself: `vane('pageview')` records the current page and `vane('pageview', {url})` a path or address on the site, whose kept query parameters (`COLLECT-002`) count as on any page load; calls made before the script loads wait in `vane.q`. A navigation to the path just recorded is not counted again. Only the document's first pageview carries the referrer, so later pageviews of the visit are direct, as after a link within the site; each pageview has its own ID, engagement pings and scroll depth (`METRIC-003`, `METRIC-006`), and page timings (`PERF-001`) belong to the document's first pageview. The settings page shows both snippet attributes. Properties on a pageview, automatic or manual, are `CONV-002`'s, which carries them in its text and acceptance. **Acceptance evidence:** Navigate a React Router and a hash-routed fixture through four views. Four pageviews arrive with the right paths and no duplicates, and a manual pageview arrives with its path and kept UTM field; its `props: {author: 'jo'}` breakdown is `CONV-002`'s acceptance.

- **INSTALL-008 — Server-side and mobile Events API.** An authenticated `POST /api/v1/events` accepts pageviews and custom events from servers, backends and mobile apps. It takes the same fields and properties as the script, plus a client IP and user agent supplied by the caller, which are used only for `METRIC-001` hashing and `DASH-005`/`DASH-006` derivation and then discarded. Tokens are scoped to one site, with rate limits and idempotency keys. The exact rules (ct 13:06Z): an owner or an administrator of the site creates its events token (`vane_e_` and 64 hex characters) on the API keys page; it is shown once, kept only as SHA-256, revocable, refused once its creator is removed, and its creation and revocation are audited. A request carries one event, or `{"events": [...]}` with 1 to 100, each of type `pageview` or `event` (a custom event naming its page load, as `CONV-002`) with the script's fields and properties plus `client_ip` and `user_agent`, and goes through the script's own collection rules (`COLLECT-001` to `COLLECT-003`, consent, paths, goals and properties). The address and agent are used in memory for the daily visitor hash, the device, browser and system classes and the owner's excluded addresses and bot rules, and never stored; the request's own Cloudflare fields (country, network, bot score) describe the caller's server, not the visitor, so they are not applied. An event naming another site is refused with 403. An `Idempotency-Key` header is kept only as its SHA-256 for 24 hours, and a repeat within them answers the first request's results without collecting again; a repeated event ID is admitted once either way. Each token may send 100 requests in each 10-second window and 10,000 events in each clock hour (UTC); over either the request is refused with 429 and Retry-After, and the limits are in the OpenAPI document. **Acceptance evidence:** Send events from a server with a site token, with a duplicate idempotency key and with a token for another site. Events count once, the foreign token is refused, and no raw IP or user agent is stored.

- **COLLECT-002 — Open path collection with automatic redaction.** Every page on a registered site is collected without a path allowlist. Each event carries the page's hostname beside its path, and the hostname must be one of the site's allowed hostnames (`SITE-001`, `DASH-013`). The current allowlist becomes an optional include or exclude list. Query strings are dropped except UTM parameters, the `ref` and `source` parameters, and owner-allowlisted keys, such as a site-search parameter (`DASH-012`). As in Plausible, a `ref` or `source` value is kept by default as the visit's source when no `utm_source` is present, after the same redaction, so directory, Product Hunt and newsletter links keep their label. Ad click identifiers (gclid, fbclid, msclkid) are recorded only as a paid-click channel flag, never as values. Path segments that look like email addresses, long tokens, UUIDs or long numbers are replaced with placeholders before storage. Owners can add rewrite rules such as `/orders/*` → `/orders/:id`. Page titles are never stored. The exact rules: the include and exclude lists hold paths or patterns with the goal wildcards (`*` within a segment, `**` across), an empty include list means every path, and a page on an excluded path is refused. The hostname comes from the page's verified origin (`SITE-001`), lowercased and without its port. Each percent-decoded path segment is replaced, in this order, by `:email` when it contains `@`, `:uuid` when it is a UUID, `:number` when it holds six or more digits in a row, and `:token` when it is 16 or more letters, digits and `-_.=` holding a digit with no `-_.`-separated part that is a word of three or more letters (so slugs such as `top-10-brews-2024` stay); rewrite rules then apply in order and the first match wins. Each kept query value is trimmed and cut to 100 characters, and one that looks like an email address, a phone or account number or a web address is dropped. A visit's source is `utm_source`, else `ref` or `source`, else the referring hostname (a referrer on one of the site's own hostnames counts as direct), else direct. Imports apply the same path, exclusion and source rules; rows on excluded paths are dropped and counted in the import report. **Acceptance evidence:** Load pages with emails, tokens, order numbers and UTM and click-ID queries in the URL. Stored paths are redacted, UTM values and the paid-click flag are kept, and a rewrite rule groups the order pages. Visits landing on `/?ref=producthunt` and `/?source=newsletter` report `producthunt` and `newsletter` as their sources, and a visit with both `utm_source=google` and `ref=x` reports `google`.

- **COLLECT-003 — Bot, referrer-spam and internal-traffic exclusion.** At ingestion Vane drops known crawlers and automation, using the Cloudflare bot score where available plus a maintained user-agent list, data-center traffic heuristics and a maintained referrer-spam list. Owners can exclude IP addresses or ranges, exclude pages, exclude up to 30 countries (ISO codes matched against Cloudflare geolocation at ingestion), and exclude their own browser. The own-browser opt-out is a link on the site's settings page that sets the `vane_ignore` flag declared in `COLLECT-001` in that browser only, and opening it again clears the flag. Excluded counts are reported by reason, never silently. The exact rules: collection refuses a page load (403) with the first matching reason in this order and adds it to that day's count for the reason (`COVERAGE-001`): an excluded IP address or CIDR range (IPv4 or IPv6, up to 50), an excluded country (up to 30 ISO 3166-1 alpha-2 codes, against Cloudflare's request country), a bot (Cloudflare bot score below 30 or a verified bot where Cloudflare provides them, else a user agent containing a fragment of the published list, which names crawlers, link previewers, monitors, headless browsers and HTTP libraries), a data-center network (Cloudflare's ASN on the published list) and referrer spam (a referring domain on the published list, or a subdomain of one). The owner's excluded addresses and countries are stored in the site's settings and its archive; the visitor's raw IP address, user agent and Cloudflare country/subdivision codes are compared in memory and never stored. Postal code, coordinates and request timezone are never stored. A refused or excluded load stores no event, visitor hash or location, only its site/day/reason/count counter. An admitted load stores only DASH-005’s country, region and city names and its US metro code; metro names live in the site's metro-name table and are never stored on events. That location travels with its event in archives and is erased with it. The automatic-event door refuses the same traffic without counting it again. While `vane_ignore` is set, each page load sends only one empty request for the site, counted as own browser, and nothing is collected. The lists are published on the methodology page (`METRIC-004`). **Acceptance evidence:** Send traffic from a headless browser, a spam referrer, an excluded IP, an excluded country and an opted-out browser beside real visits. Only the real visits count, and the exclusion report shows each reason and its count.

- **COLLECT-004 — Collection at small-business scale.** One installation collects at least 10 million pageviews a month across its sites with bursts of 500 events per second. Dashboards answer a 12-month query in under 2 seconds at that volume, using aggregated rollups and a declared analytical store (Cloudflare Analytics Engine, D1 rollups or the buyer's warehouse through `API-002`). Any limit is published and shown before it is reached: the current fixed 10,000-events-per-site-per-day admission ceiling is lifted. **Acceptance evidence:** A load test at the stated volume and burst rate admits every valid event exactly once. The 12-month dashboard query is timed, and the limit warning appears at the published threshold.

- **COVERAGE-001 — Coverage shown beside every number.** Every report states what it cannot see: the consent-declined share (the script sends a field-free declined counter when consent is not granted, if the owner enables it), sampling, exclusions by reason, the proxy and direct share, imported periods with unknown coverage, and deleted history. Where the site's Cloudflare zone is connected, Vane also compares its pageviews with zone page requests to estimate the share lost to blockers. No total is presented as complete when coverage is unknown. The exact rules: each share is its count over the page loads that reached Vane for it (that count plus the accepted page loads in the range); the declined-consent and exclusion counts hold only the site, day and reason; erased history is named with the day before which events were erased; and since blocked scripts are never measured until a zone is connected, every total is labeled partial. This is done when every coverage gap that exists today is shown: declined consent, privacy signals, excluded paths, sampling, imports with unknown coverage, erased history and page loads refused as collection errors (`ALERT-002`). Each later source of a gap shows its share before it ships: bot, referrer-spam, IP, country and own-browser exclusions (`COLLECT-003`), the proxy and direct share (`INSTALL-006`) and the Cloudflare zone comparison when a zone connection exists. The counts are site-wide under a Hostname filter (`DASH-013`). **Acceptance evidence:** Combine declined consent, sampling, exclusions, an import and a zone comparison on one site. The report shows each share with its denominator and labels the totals as partial.

### Visitors and engagement

- **METRIC-001 — Cookie-free unique visitors from daily-rotating salted hashes.** The server derives a visitor hash from SHA-256(daily salt + site ID + IP address + user agent). The salt is random, rotates at midnight in the site's time zone and is deleted after 24 hours. The IP and user agent are never stored, logged or returned. Unique visitors count distinct hashes within a day. For a multi-day range, the count is the sum of daily uniques unless consented mode (`METRIC-005`) applies, and the methodology page says so. A cookie-free visitor cannot be followed across days or across sites. The separate explicitly consented mode (`METRIC-005`) can link days within one site only. **Acceptance evidence:** Send pageviews from two simulated visitors over two days. Unique visitors are 2 per day and the range shows 4 under the stated method. Show that the database, logs and archive hold no IP, no user agent and no salt older than 24 hours, and that the same visitor hashes differently on another site.

- **METRIC-002 — Visits and pages per visit.** A visit is a run of events from one visitor hash with no gap over the site's visit timeout, 30 minutes by default (the owner may approve another from 15 to 120 minutes as a dated definition version, `METRIC-004`); it also ends when the daily salt rotates. Reports show visits, pageviews, pages per visit and views per visitor. **Acceptance evidence:** Send one visitor's pageviews with gaps of 10, 40 and 5 minutes. The report shows 2 visits, 4 pageviews and 2 pages per visit.

- **METRIC-003 — Bounce rate and visit duration.** A bounce is a visit with one pageview and no engagement event. Visit duration runs from the first event to the last engagement signal. The script sends a field-free engagement ping when the page is hidden or after 10 seconds or more of visible time, which counts time on the final page. Reports show the bounce rate and the median and average visit duration, per page as well as overall. **Acceptance evidence:** Simulate a quick bounce, a single-page visit with 45 seconds of engagement, and a three-page visit. The bounce rate, visit durations and per-page time match the definitions.

- **METRIC-004 — Methodology disclosed and versioned.** Every installation serves a public methodology page. It defines visitors, visits, bounce, duration, scroll depth, conversions and revenue, the hash recipe and salt lifetime, consented mode, the two declared browser-storage exceptions (`COLLECT-001`), how each metric combines in a multi-site roll-up (`DASH-010`), sampling and estimation limits. Each metric in the app links to its definition. A change to a definition is versioned and appears as an annotation on every chart it affects (`DASH-009`). The exact rules: the one definition an owner can change today is the visit timeout (`METRIC-002`), a whole number of minutes from 15 to 120, 30 by default, changed only through the owner's approval (`APPROVE-001`). An approved change is the site's next visit-definition version (the published definitions are version 1), dated the site's local day of approval; it applies to whole local days from that date, earlier days keep the version they were counted under, and a roll-up applies each member site's own version. It is annotated with its date and the old and new timeouts on every chart of visits, bounce rate or visit duration whose range covers that day, naming the site in a roll-up; unique visitors, pageviews and conversions do not depend on it, so their charts carry no such annotation. Every metric total in the app and on public stats pages links to its definition's anchor on the methodology page. Vane makes no estimates: sampled counts are never scaled up and nothing is modeled for blocked scripts or declined consent. Revenue is defined as `CONV-006` records it; consented mode is defined by `METRIC-005`: its explicit consent, random first-party identifier, fixed owner-approved lifetime, site-scoped digest, returning-visitor and cohort definitions, attribution and revocation are published there. The location entry says that metro codes are Nielsen DMA® codes from Cloudflare, that Vane ships no metro names and a site's names come from its owner, and that “DMA® is a registered service mark of The Nielsen Company; Vane is not affiliated with or endorsed by Nielsen” (`DASH-005`). **Acceptance evidence:** Open the methodology page signed out and follow each metric's definition link from the dashboard. The page states the roll-up rules and the two storage exceptions. Change the visit timeout and see a definition annotation dated on the charts.

- **METRIC-005 — Consented visitor mode: returning visitors and retention.** A site can turn on a consented mode for visitors who grant analytics consent. It stores a random first-party identifier with a lifetime the owner chooses (at most 13 months) and adds new and returning visitors, multi-day unique visitors, multi-visit attribution and weekly retention cohorts, as in Matomo. Visitors without consent stay in cookie-free mode, and reports show the consented share. Revoking consent deletes the identifier on the device and stops linking. Before the mode is offered, the methodology page (`METRIC-004`) replaces its "consented mode does not exist yet" entry with the identifier, its lifetime and how returning visitors and cohorts are counted. In consented mode a funnel can order its steps within one visitor across visits (`CONV-005`), and a split test (`EXPERIMENT-001`) keeps a consented visitor's variant across days, assigned from the identifier instead of the daily hash. The exact rules: mode is off by default and changes use the owner's recorded settings approval (`APPROVE-001`). The owner chooses 1–13 whole calendar months (13 only if that proposal is approved), fixed from issuance, never extended by visits, with a short month's last day used when needed. Explicit analytics consent is required even where anonymous collection needs no consent; denied or unknown consent uses cookie-free collection only where the site's existing regional rule allows it. One first-party `vane_consent_<site ID>` localStorage entry holds a random UUID and its issue time. Only a site-scoped SHA-256 digest and issue/expiry times are stored with accepted events; raw identifier values never enter database, logs, reports or archives, and no digest is exposed in a report or ordinary export. Revocation through `vane('consent', false)` or `vane:consent` with false detail immediately clears the device entry and pending linked page references; a later grant creates a new identity. CMP updates must notify Vane; GPC/DNT and expiry stop linkage too. Historical digests remain only with their events until ordinary retention or erasure removes them. New visitors are first observed in retained site history during the selected range; returning visitors have a later visit, possibly on that same day, so a visitor may be both. Consented range uniques are deduplicated per site and combined with the sum of cookie-free daily uniques; daily buckets need not sum to a multi-day unique total. The consented share is identifier-bearing accepted native page loads divided by all accepted native page loads, excluding imports and rejected traffic. Cohorts group visitors first observed in the range by the Monday of their first site-local week, with distinct active visitors in each subsequent week over the original cohort size; unfinished weeks are labelled partial, never estimated. Multi-visit attribution shows converting visits under first touch and last non-direct touch using retained acquisition before conversion. A funnel marked `@visitor` orders a consented visitor's steps across visits within the range; unlinked visitors retain their within-visit semantics. Experiments assign from the site-scoped digest while it exists, and from the daily hash otherwise. Every roll-up still counts each member site independently (`DASH-010`). Cohort and attribution tables are aggregate-only private report/CSV data, with no identifier on shared or public pages. **Acceptance evidence:** With consent granted, visit on day 1 and day 8 and see one returning visitor in the week-2 cohort. Without consent, the same visits count as cookie-free daily visitors. A revoke deletes the identifier. The methodology page (`METRIC-004`) defines consented mode, its identifier and lifetime. A consented visitor who views the product on day 1 and buys on day 3 completes a cross-visit funnel, and sees the same split-test variant on both days.

- **METRIC-006 — Scroll depth and time on page.** The script reports the maximum scroll depth reached on each page as a whole percentage (0 to 100), as part of the engagement ping. Page reports show the average scroll depth, the share reaching 75%, and the time on page, and scroll-depth goals (`CONV-001`) count against the same figure. **Acceptance evidence:** Scroll a long fixture page to 50% and to 100% in separate visits. The page shows the average depth and the 75% share as expected.

### Dashboards and analysis

- **DASH-001 — Real-time dashboard.** A real-time view shows current visitors (active in the last 5 minutes), pageviews per minute for the last 30 minutes, and live top pages, sources and locations, refreshing at least every 15 seconds without a reload. The exact rules: a visitor is current while their latest page load or engagement ping (`METRIC-003`) is under 5 minutes old; pageviews per minute fill 30 one-minute buckets in the site's time zone ending with the current, still running minute; the live pages list counts each current visitor at their latest page load, and the live sources list counts them under the named source (`DASH-014`) of their visit's first page load (`DASH-004`). As `DASH-008` requires, every figure and list is compared with the same window 30 minutes earlier, and the chart overlays the 30 minutes before as a dashed line. The view's one nonce'd script fetches the same session-authenticated page every 10 seconds and swaps in the new figures, so only this view's Content Security Policy allows `connect-src 'self'`; every other page keeps `'none'`. The same figures are served at `GET /api/vane/realtime`. This is done when every dimension that exists today is live; location dimensions add their live locations list when `DASH-005` ships, which carries it. **Acceptance evidence:** Send events while the real-time view is open. - **DASH-002 — Historical time series in the site's time zone.** The main chart plots visitors, visits, pageviews, bounce rate, duration, conversions and revenue by hour, day, week or month. Preset ranges cover today, yesterday, the last 7, 30 and 90 days, month to date, last month, year to date, the last 12 months and all time, and any custom range can be chosen. Days start at midnight in the site's time zone, which defaults to the owner's US time zone and handles daylight-saving changes. Every chart has an accessible data table. The exact rules: a range is local dates, end exclusive; the last 7, 30 and 90 days end with today, month and year to date run through today, the last 12 months start on the first of the month eleven months before this one, and all time starts on the site's earliest event day; filled From and Until dates make a custom range. The chart defaults to hourly up to 2 days, daily up to 92, weekly up to 731 and monthly beyond; hourly covers at most 31 days and daily at most two years. An hour is a real hour from local midnight, labeled with its local time and zone abbreviation, so a fall-back day has 25 hours (01:00 CDT, then 01:00 CST) and a spring-forward day 23; weeks start on Monday, and the first and last weeks and months are cut to the range. A bucket's visitors are its days' daily unique visitors summed (an hour counts its distinct visitor hashes); a visit, its bounce and its duration (the chart plots the average) belong to the bucket of its first page load; pageviews and goal conversions count where they happened. Imported history has dates but no times, so it appears in daily, weekly and monthly buckets and not hourly ones. The add-site form fills in the time zone the owner's browser reports; a site prepared without one, such as through the command API, uses UTC until the owner changes it. This is done when every metric that exists today is charted, revenue included: it is charted in the site currency in the bucket of the page load it was sent on (`CONV-006`). **Acceptance evidence:** Seed events either side of a US daylight-saving change and midnight in America/Chicago. The daily buckets follow local days, each preset range returns the expected totals, and the data table matches the chart.

- **DASH-003 — Page, entry and exit reports.** Page reports list top pages, entry (landing) pages and exit pages, each with visitors, pageviews, bounce rate, time on page, scroll depth and conversion rate. Pages can be grouped by path prefix or by a rewrite rule. When a site has more than one hostname, each row shows its hostname and the same path on two hostnames is two rows (`DASH-013`). **Acceptance evidence:** Seed visits with known entry and exit pages. The three tables match, and grouping by `/blog/*` sums the posts correctly.

- **DASH-004 — Channels, referrers and UTM campaigns.** Every visit gets a channel, using published definitions: Direct, Organic Search, Paid Search, Organic Social, Paid Social, Email, Referral, AI Assistants (ChatGPT, Perplexity, Claude, Gemini, Copilot), Affiliates and Display. Referrer domains and URLs come from public referring pages. All five UTM fields (source, medium, campaign, term, content) are available as dimensions. No allowlist is needed. Email-client and webmail referrers are grouped. Every channel, referrer and UTM table shows, per row, the visitors, visits, bounce rate, visit duration and the conversion rate for the selected goal, as the pages table does. The exact rules: the channel is set at collection by the first matching rule, in the order Affiliates, Display, Paid Social, Paid Search, AI Assistants, Email, Organic Search, Organic Social, Referral, Direct, from the visit's source (`COLLECT-002`), `utm_medium` and paid-click flag, with the source lists and the paid `utm_medium` values published on the methodology page (`METRIC-004`). A referring page is kept as its hostname and path, redacted like page paths, without query or fragment; email-client and webmail referrers are grouped as the source `webmail` and their addresses are never kept. Each visit counts under its first page load's channel, source, referring page and UTM values. The conversion rate counts visits with the goal the reader selects, or with any goal when none is selected; the page tables follow the same selection. Imported history has no visits, so it is left out of these tables. **Acceptance evidence:** Send visits with Google organic, Google paid (gclid flag), Facebook, an email UTM, a chatgpt.com referrer and five-field UTMs. Each lands in its documented channel, every UTM field is reportable, and each row's visitors, bounce rate and visit duration equal a hand-computed count of the seeded visits.

- **DASH-005 — Country, region and city.** Location comes from Cloudflare's request geolocation at ingestion. Country, region (US state) and city are stored as names; the US metro area (DMA) is stored as its code and named from the site's own metro-name table; none of it is ever stored as an IP address. On shared links, embeds and public dashboards (`SHARE-001`, `SHARE-002`), a city with fewer than 5 visitors in the selected range is folded into an "Other cities" row in its region. Signed-in team members see every city. Country, region and city rows show conversions and conversion rate for the selected goal (`CONV-004`). Countries, regions and cities join the movers in the report's explanation (`REPORT-003`) and are filter dimensions (`DASH-007`). The real-time view (`DASH-001`) gains a live locations list: current visitors by country, region and city, compared with the same window 30 minutes earlier. The exact rules: the country is stored as its English name from Cloudflare's two-letter code (unknown `XX` and Tor `T1` store no location), the region is Cloudflare's first-level subdivision name (a US state, a Canadian province, England), and the US metro area is stored as `metro_code` only when a US page load carries exactly three ASCII digits (otherwise NULL); events never store a metro name. By ct ruling (20 October 2026) Vane ships no DMA code-to-name table, pinned or fetched, because no published source licenses one for this MIT repository (`SOURCES.md`): names come only from the site's own metro-name table, a site setting holding no visitor data. A signed-in owner or administrator of the site uploads it on the site's settings page as a UTF-8 CSV with the header `metro_code,metro_name`, at most 1,000 rows and 262,144 bytes (256 KB): each code exactly three digits and listed once, each name 1 to 100 characters after trimming, with no control character and not starting with `=`, `+`, `-` or `@`. The upload carries a source label of 1 to 200 characters, the owner's statement “This site may use and show these names under the terms of their source” and a switch, off by default, to also show the names on shared links, embeds and public pages. Viewers, Analysts, Stats API and provisioning keys, shared-link viewers and the agent command API cannot prepare one, and there is no import from a web address. Any error refuses the whole file, naming its rows (counted from the first row after the header), and changes nothing. The owner reviews a preview (rows; added, changed and removed names; codes seen in the site's events that it names or leaves unnamed; its codes never seen) and only the owner approves it (`APPROVE-001`). Approval replaces the whole table at once on every surface, older data included, and is audited (`AUDIT-001`); it is refused (409) when another approval changed the table after this one was prepared, and an approved clear brings back the unnamed labels. The table travels with the archive, restore and site transfer (`ARCHIVE-001`, `SITE-001`), erasure and retention leave it alone, deleting the site removes it, and it is never sent anywhere. A name is looked up when a report, export or API answer is made: the site's table, then a bundled table only under a written licence recorded first in `SOURCES.md` (none exists), then unnamed; a roll-up (`DASH-010`) names a code from its member sites' tables, the first member's name first. Filters, saved segments, comparisons and movers key on the code (`metro:is:635`). A named code shows its name. An unnamed code shows “Metro NNN (unnamed)” with “Most visitors: ‹city›, ‹region›”: the city with the most unique visitors among that metro's visits in the range, counted as the report counts (each visit's first page load, daily uniques summed), ties broken alphabetically, the region alone when no city is known and nothing when neither is. That place is display only: it is never stored, never put in `metro_name`, never exported and never returned by the Stats API, MCP or the session JSON doors, and it is never called the DMA's name. A US page load without a valid code counts as “Unknown metro area”, a page load without geolocation stays in the “Unknown location” row of the country, region and city tables, and non-US page loads are not in the US metro areas table. Public pages fold metros under five visitors into “Other metro areas”, show the owner's names only once the owner approved them for public pages (otherwise the unnamed label), and name an unnamed code's city only when that city's own row is shown there (5 or more unique visitors), otherwise its region. Wherever the owner's names show, a footnote reads “Metro names: ‹label›, supplied by the site owner”; the signed-in report counts its unnamed codes in a footnote linking to the setting, and the settings page shows the source label, approval date, approver, row count, the public choice and the codes seen without a name. Headings say “US metro areas”. A page load without Cloudflare geolocation, and a server-side Events API send (`INSTALL-008`), whose request location is the sender's server, stores NULL in every location field and appears in an explicit “Unknown location” row. Raw IP address, user agent, Cloudflare country/subdivision codes, postal code, coordinates and request timezone are never stored. Refused, excluded, consent-denied, DNT and GPC loads retain only site/day/reason/count counters when enabled. Location travels with its event in the archive and is erased with it. Each visit counts under its first page load's location, like the channel and device tables (`DASH-004`, `CONV-004`), and a location filter judges that first page load. The fold threshold counts unique visitors as the report does (daily uniques summed over the range); folded cities sum into one "Other cities" row per region. Locations appear on a shared link or the public stats page only when the owner chooses the "Countries, regions and cities" report (`SHARE-002`: none is chosen by default). Movers rank locations by unique visitors. **Acceptance evidence:** Send events with Cloudflare geo for Texas, Ontario and London. - **DASH-006 — Devices, browsers and operating systems.** The server derives the device class (desktop, mobile, tablet), browser and major version, operating system and version, and a screen-size class from the user agent and an optional viewport width. The raw user agent is discarded. **Acceptance evidence:** Send events from iPhone Safari, Android Chrome, Windows Edge and an iPad. - **DASH-007 — Filters, segments and saved views.** Clicking any dimension value filters the whole dashboard. Filters combine with is, is not, contains, does not contain and matches (regex), on any dimension (including Hostname, `DASH-013`) or event property. Filter combinations can be saved as named segments, shared with the team, and used in reports, alerts and the API. A saved segment also filters a funnel (`CONV-005`). The exact rules: a filter keeps or drops whole visits, so every total, table, chart, journey (`DASH-011`), funnel and explanation of a filtered report is computed over the page loads and goal events of the kept visits. Channel, source, referring page, UTM fields, entry page, device, browser and its version, operating system and its version, and screen size judge each visit's first page load; page, hostname, site-search term and goal keep a visit when any of its page loads matches (is, contains, matches) or when none does (is not, does not contain). Filters combine with AND, after the Hostname filter of `DASH-013`. is and is not compare exactly, and on Page and Entry page they take the goal wildcards of `CONV-001`; contains and does not contain ignore ASCII case; matches is a JavaScript regular expression tested against each distinct value in the range (at most 500 matching values). A report takes up to 10 filters with values of up to 200 characters. Imported history and automatic events carry no visit, so a filtered report leaves them out, and coverage counts stay site-wide. Each later clause carries its part before it ships: event properties become filter dimensions with `CONV-002`, country, region and city with `DASH-005`, alerts take a saved segment with `ALERT-001`, and the versioned Stats API takes filters and segments with `API-001`. **Acceptance evidence:** From the report, filter by Organic Search and mobile and not `/blog/*`, save the segment and reopen it in the report and the summary API. Each total equals a hand-computed count of the seeded events, and each comparison keeps whole visits. The saved segment recomputes a funnel's step counts. A member applies the segment but cannot save one. Reopening the segment in an alert is `ALERT-001`'s acceptance.

- **DASH-008 — Period comparisons.** Any range can be compared with the previous period, the same period last year or a custom period. Both series appear on the chart, and every table shows the change with its absolute and percentage values. Comparisons match weekdays when the owner chooses. The exact rules: the previous period is the same number of days just before the range; last year is the same calendar dates a year earlier (29 February becomes 1 March); a custom period is any start and end dates. Matching weekdays moves the previous period back by the range's length rounded up to whole weeks, last year back by 364 days, and a custom period back by 0 to 6 days, so each compared start falls on the range's starting weekday. ### Conversions and revenue

- **CONV-001 — Pageview goals with patterns and conversion rates.** A goal can be a single path or a wildcard pattern with Plausible's meaning: `*` matches within one path segment and `**` across segments (`/thank-you*`, `/thanks/*`, `/blog/**`). Reports show conversions, unique converting visitors and the conversion rate against visitors. A goal can also be a scroll-depth goal: visitors reaching N% (any whole percentage) of a page or pattern, such as 75% on `/blog/**`, counted from `METRIC-006`, with the same uniques and conversion rate. Plausible's scroll-depth goals import as these (`IMPORT-008`). **Acceptance evidence:** Define `/thanks/*` and `/thanks**` and visit `/thanks/pro` and `/thanks/pro/annual`. `/thanks/*` converts on `/thanks/pro` only (`*` stays within one path segment, as in Plausible), `/thanks**` converts on both, and the conversion rate equals converting visitors divided by visitors. Define a 75% scroll goal on `/blog/**`. A visitor scrolling a post to 80% converts and one scrolling to 50% does not.

- **CONV-002 — Custom events with properties.** Sites send events through `vane('Signup', {props: {plan: 'pro'}})` or by adding CSS classes (`vane-event-name=Signup`, with property classes). An event can carry up to 30 properties with string or number values. Property keys and values pass the `COLLECT-002` redaction, and an event carrying an email address is refused. Properties are dimensions for filtering (`DASH-007` filters and saved segments), breakdowns and funnels. Pageviews, automatic or sent with `vane('pageview', {url, props})`, carry properties the same way, for example author or category on a blog post (`INSTALL-007`). The script also accepts the Plausible and Fathom call and class syntax, so a switched site keeps counting before any code changes (`IMPORT-008`). A funnel step can be a custom event with property conditions (`CONV-005`). The exact rules: a custom event (`POST /api/vane/custom`) names the page load it happened on, so it belongs to that page load's visitor hash, day and visit, and no IP address or user agent is read for it; the script sends it on the current page load, after that page load's pageview was accepted. A class event fires on a click of the element carrying `vane-event-name=<name>` (or on submit of such a form), with `vane-event-<key>=<value>` classes as properties and `+` for a space. Automatic pageviews take properties from `data-prop-<name>` attributes on the snippet. An event or pageview carries at most 30 properties; keys are 1 to 40 lowercase letters, digits, `_` or `-`; values are strings of 1 to 100 characters or finite numbers, and event names 1 to 60 characters of plain text. A key, value or name that looks like an email address refuses the whole event (400); a string value that looks like a phone or account number or a web address is dropped and counted per site and day as a dropped property in the report's coverage (`COVERAGE-001`); kept strings take the `COLLECT-002` path redaction. Each event ID is accepted once, and excluded traffic (`COLLECT-003`) is refused. The report lists custom events with their events and visitors, event properties by event, key and value, and pageview properties by key and value, each with its compared-period change (`DASH-008`), and each value filters the report: `event` and `prop.<key>` are `DASH-007` dimensions that keep a visit when any of its page loads, or a custom event on one, matches. A funnel step `event:<name>` or `event:<name>[key=value]` may be reached on the page load of the step before it. The data inventory and generated policy name both tables; public stats pages (`SHARE-002`) show no custom event or property. **Acceptance evidence:** Fire Signup with plan=pro and plan=free through JavaScript and CSS classes. The breakdown by plan matches, and a property holding an email address is refused. A manual pageview (`INSTALL-007`) with author=jo breaks down by author. - **CONV-006 — Revenue goals.** A custom event can carry a revenue amount and an ISO currency. - **EXPERIMENT-001 — A/B tests with honest results.** An owner sets up a split test of 2 to 4 variants on a page. Assignment is stable within a visitor's day in cookie-free mode, and across days in consented mode. The site applies a variant through the script API, or through Pane or the buyer's code. Results show each variant's visitors, conversions and conversion rate with a 95% interval, the probability that it beats control, a minimum-sample warning, and a sample-ratio-mismatch check. A test cannot be called while it is underpowered. The exact rules: tests are part of the site settings, one per line as `pricing = /pricing > signup : control, cheaper` (a page path or pattern with the goal wildcards, one of the site's goals, then 2 to 4 variant names, the first being the control; at most 10), so administrators prepare them and the owner approves them (`APPROVE-001`). In cookie-free mode a visitor's variant is the first four bytes of SHA-256(the daily visitor hash, `:`, the test name) modulo the number of variants, so it is stable within the visitor's day, equal across variants in expectation, and needs nothing stored; an accepted page load on the test's page returns its variant, `vane('experiment', 'pricing', variant => ...)` hands it to the page once that pageview is accepted, and the script then reports the exposure (`POST /api/vane/exposure`), whose variant the server recomputes from the page load's own hash and never takes from the request, once per page load and test. A variant's visitors are the distinct daily visitor hashes exposed to it, summed over days, and its conversions those visitor-days reaching the test's goal at or after their first exposure that day; its conversion rate has a 95% Wilson interval. Each other variant shows its difference from the control with a 95% normal interval and the probability that it beats the control, Φ(difference ÷ standard error). The minimum sample is the visitors per variant needed to detect a 20% relative lift on the control's rate with 80% power at a two-sided 5% level; until every variant has it, or while the control has no conversion, the test is underpowered and its result is not called, and once called a variant beats or trails the control only when its difference's interval excludes zero. The sample-ratio check compares the variants' exposed visitors with an equal split by a chi-square test and warns below p = 0.01. Results follow the report's range and filters (`DASH-007`); a roll-up shows no tests. This is done when cookie-free tests applied through the script API conform; assignment across days ships with consented mode (`METRIC-005`) and applying a variant from Pane with Pane's switch (`INSTALL-004`), each carrying it in its text and acceptance before it is done. **Acceptance evidence:** Run a fixture test with a seeded 20% lift and a null test. The first reaches significance with the expected interval, the null test never does, and a skewed split triggers the mismatch warning. Before every variant has the minimum sample the test reports underpowered and calls no result. In a browser, `vane('experiment', ...)` hands the page its variant and reports the exposure, and an exposure naming its own variant is refused (`tests/vane.test.mjs`).

## Push further with agents

These agents work on the site's own analytics, its connected outcome sources and the grants the owner gives. They go beyond chat over charts: they keep the measurement trustworthy, act within granted authority and measure what happened. Every agent action is audited (`AUDIT-001`), and every change an agent ships is annotated (`DASH-009`) and can be reversed. No agent may add an identifier, bypass consent, change a metric definition silently, invent revenue, or match identities across sites.

- **AGENT-001 — Maintain trustworthy instrumentation.** The agent compares the declared goals, events and funnels with what actually arrives. It also reads the site's pages (by crawling the sitemap), deploy diffs from a connected GitHub repository or Pane, the consent state, and server-side counts from Stripe, Shopify or the CRM (`CONV-008`). It finds missing, duplicated, misnamed and newly broken events, pages without the script, and funnels whose steps no longer exist. For each problem it drafts a fix: a pull request to the site repository, a GTM template change, a Pane setting or a no-code event (`CONV-009`). After the fix ships it verifies the real payload. A human approves each fix, and the agent never widens the collected fields. Implemented conservative default for GitHub authority, ct approved as adopted with riders, 22 Oct: off until explicit audited owner consent; one repository/base and mapped templates; a GitHub App installation or fine-grained token restricted to contents and pull requests on the mapped repository (no administration, actions/workflows write or secrets), sealed with AES-GCM and excluded from archives; read-only source/diff bodies kept only in memory; normal version-bound approval of the exact diff hash (any diff change voids approval) plus a separate explicit owner action before any script-only HTML draft PR; one PR per approved fix on a namespaced Vane branch only, no force push, never a base/default-branch update, merge or deploy; revocation or an ownership change blocks further source reads/writes, a new owner reconnects explicitly, and uncertain outcomes reconcile read-only. CI/workflow files, deploy config, dependency manifests and secret-bearing paths require the owner to change them by hand and never enter an agent PR. `.env` and key-like paths are never read. Repository content never goes to a model provider without AGENT-007 per-site consent; this implementation makes no source-to-model request. AGENT-003 inherits source-generation, approval, revocation and no-replay fences before adding broader granted edits. Current native door: the owner explicitly starts a bounded sitemap investigation, reviews structural candidates, prepares a normal version-bound no-code proposal and verifies a deployed fix against new accepted page and event payloads. ## Honest boundaries

- **No identity graph, fingerprinting or ad-exchange attribution.** Vane measures first-party traffic under declared modes. It never builds cross-site identities, fingerprints or ad audiences, and it does not place ad pixels. Buyers who need platform conversion tracking keep the ad platform's own tag, through GTM. Vane reads campaign spend under `ADS-001` and changes campaigns only under an `AGENT-003` grant.
- **"Cookieless" is not a legal guarantee.** Vane shows its exact fields, identifier lifetimes and consent behavior (`PRIVACY-001`) and supports consent managers (`PRIVACY-003`). The owner and their counsel choose the mode. Vane does not certify compliance with GDPR, ePrivacy, CCPA/CPRA or any other state law.
- **Counts are estimates with stated coverage.** Visitors are hashed daily estimates, not people. Revenue and leads are confirmed only through the connected Stripe, Shopify, WooCommerce, Stall, CRM and form sources (`CONV-008`), and causal lift is claimed only under an experiment (`EXPERIMENT-001`). Blocked and declined traffic is estimated and disclosed, never filled in.
- **No individual session replay or per-visitor log.** Vane offers aggregate heatmaps, scroll maps, journeys and funnels. It has no per-visit record of one person's actions, such as Matomo's Visits Log and Visitor Profile, because it keeps no per-person history. Journeys (`DASH-011`) answer the path questions in aggregate. A buyer who needs per-visitor recordings uses PostHog, Microsoft Clarity or Hotjar under their own consent; Vane links to it from annotations and does not store recordings.
- **No logged-in product analytics with person profiles.** Website and app traffic through the Events API is in scope. Per-user product analytics, feature flags and person profiles stay in PostHog, Amplitude or Mixpanel. Vane's webhooks and warehouse export (`API-002`, `API-003`) feed them.
- **Tag management and SEO tooling are not rebuilt.** Third-party marketing tags stay in Google Tag Manager, which Vane installs through (`INSTALL-005`). Backlink, rank-tracking and site-audit suites (Ahrefs, Semrush) stay separate. Vane reads Search Console and Bing keyword data.
- **Neighboring jobs stay with their products.** Website building is Pane, storefronts are Stall or Shopify, CRM is Orbit or HubSpot, forms are Pencil, bookings are Dial, email campaigns are Mango, uptime is Uptime, errors are Spool, feedback is Penny and automation is Crank. Vane integrates with each of them. See [Porthole (sibling seed)](https://github.com/just-every/run-porthole).
- **Raw event storage has a capacity design.** History is stored as the rollups and anonymous events declared in `COLLECT-004` within published limits. Unlimited raw history goes to the buyer's warehouse (`API-002`).
- **US first.** USD reporting, US time zones and daylight saving, US federal holidays in baselines, US state privacy signals and a US data location are the defaults. Other currencies, regions and the EU jurisdiction are supported, but not first.

## Declared data flows

First-party proxy setup (`INSTALL-006`, existing required `seed.json` external `cloudflare`) sends only explicit active-owner configuration requests to the fixed `https://api.cloudflare.com/client/v4/` API, with redirects refused. The API receives the one-use owner token, account and zone IDs, the existing installation Worker name, and the chosen hostname/path; it returns zone/Worker metadata and route or dedicated-domain resource IDs. No collected Vane visitor rows are sent. The token is transient and never saved, logged, exported or echoed; only the connected resource configuration and owner/action/time audit are retained. Other routes and existing DNS records are never overwritten. External server proxy traffic goes only to the owner’s installation with a site-scoped server credential, never to a Vane-operated service.

Tracking cut-over scan (`IMPORT-008`) is the explicit owner/admin-started extension of Verify installation: GET the registered origin’s robots.txt, its listed sitemaps or /sitemap.xml, and listed pages on that same origin. An owner-selected page limit bounds the scan. Robots disallow, credentials and redirects are refused. No cookies, authorization or visitor records are sent, no fetched body is kept, and only derived install/capture findings with URL/time plus actor/time/fetched/refused counts are stored. No seed.json integration entry is needed for the owner’s own origin.

REPORT-002 optionally sends aggregate report/alert figures and a private filtered dashboard link to owner-connected Slack channels or Teams workflows or Nook channels (Nook `NOOK-079` scoped integration token). Nook receives only aggregate figures and the private link in a message body with a stable idempotency key; its owner-entered origin follows the same EGRESS RULE. Slack receives its OAuth code and chat:write bot token only at fixed official endpoints. Teams receives POST Adaptive Cards at the owner-entered HTTPS workflow URL with no redirects, credentials in URLs refused, under API-002’s literal/name public-host rule. No visitor/person fields leave. Tokens and webhook URLs stay sealed and out of archives. Teams acceptance is acceptance by workflow, channel delivery unconfirmed. Resolved-address/pinned egress awaits the Base egress door.


Today Vane receives only page-load events from registered origins, sends only sign-in and invitation mail through the configured Base mail adapter, and declares Cloudflare as its only external in `seed.json`. Verify installation (`INSTALL-001`) sends one GET for the site's homepage at its registered origin, following up to five redirects. It carries no visitor data, keeps nothing beyond the pass or fail result it reports to the owner, and needs no `seed.json` externals entry because it reads the owner's own public page. The no-code event preview (`CONV-009`) likewise sends one GET for a page an owner or administrator names at the site's registered origins, following up to five redirects that stay on them; it carries no visitor data and keeps nothing. The scheduled warehouse export (`API-002`, `seed.json` external `warehouse`) is the first connected integration: only when the owner sets it up, the Worker sends signed PUT requests to the owner's own S3-compatible bucket at a public https endpoint, carrying each ended date's dashboard totals and event rows without any visitor hash, salt, IP address or user agent; its scope is write access to the named bucket and prefix, it receives nothing back but the upload result, and its secret key is encrypted at rest, revocable by stopping the export and excluded from archives. Webhooks (`API-003`, `seed.json` external `webhooks`) are the second: only when an owner or administrator adds an endpoint, the Worker POSTs signed JSON to that public https address carrying per-minute goal conversion counts and never a visitor-level field; it receives only the HTTP status, and the signing secret is encrypted at rest, revoked by deleting the webhook and excluded from archives. Browser push services (`MOBILE-001`, `seed.json` external `push`) are the third: only when a signed-in person turns on alerts in a browser, the Worker POSTs to that browser's push-service endpoint (a public https address the browser's vendor runs) a message encrypted for that browser (RFC 8291) and signed with the installation's VAPID key (RFC 8292), carrying an alert title, a site name and a link into the app and never a visitor-level field; it receives only the HTTP status. The VAPID private key is encrypted at rest; a subscription is deleted when its person turns alerts off, is removed from the team or the push service answers 404 or 410; keys and subscriptions are excluded from archives. Matomo (`IMPORT-005`, optional `seed.json` external `matomo`) is read only when the owner initiates an import: the Worker POSTs `API.getBulkRequest` to the owner-entered public https Reporting API address, with the source site ID, selected local day, known report methods and a one-use token with view access; redirects are refused. It receives only aggregate reports and goal definitions, never visitor logs. Only sanitized dimensions, numeric measures and definitions enter import custody; the token and raw response are discarded before custody and are absent from previews, journals and archives. The owner-enabled plain-English questions (`AGENT-007`, optional `seed.json` external `models`) are the fourth: only when the owner turns questions on for a site and the deployment configures a model provider through the Base `ai` module (`AI_ENABLED`, `AI_MODEL`, `AI_API_KEY`, optional `AI_BASE_URL`), the Worker POSTs one chat request for each question to that provider’s https address, carrying the question text, Vane’s metric, breakdown, filter and period names, the site’s goal names, time zone and today’s date, and never a stored Vane analytics figure, visitor-level field, page value or identifier. Typed question text beyond email is not automatically stripped of personal data, as the ask-box notice states; deployment-provider retention and training terms remain an external Gap. It receives one JSON query plan, which Vane validates and discards after running it; Vane stores no question, reply or answer. The provider key is a deployment secret outside the database, archives and history; a rejected call is not retried and no second provider is tried. The owner-connected instrumentation GitHub source (`AGENT-001`, optional `seed.json` external `github`) is off until explicit audited consent: only fixed `https://api.github.com` endpoints receive the repository-scoped credential, repository/base/path identifiers and, after normal fix approval plus a separate explicit owner action, a script-only HTML edit returned to the same repository on a new Vane branch and draft PR. Source and deploy-diff bodies are read in memory and discarded. No analytics figure, visitor field, new collection field or raw source body enters Vane custody. The token is AES-GCM-sealed, bound to the site/connection generation, revocable by disconnect/rotation and excluded from archives; pending outbound PR authority is also excluded. Vane never updates the base branch, merges or deploys. Lost mutation responses are reconciled with reads, never automatically replayed. Each target integration is a new outbound data flow. These are Google Search Console, Google Analytics, Bing, ad platforms, Stripe, Shopify, WooCommerce, HubSpot, Slack, Microsoft Teams, warehouses, consent managers, Pane, Stall, Orbit, Pencil, Dial, Mango, Uptime, Spool, Nook, Crank, GitHub, Webflow and MCP clients. Each one ships only with its `seed.json` externals entry, its scopes, the data it sends and receives, and token custody (encrypted at rest, revocable and excluded from archives) declared in this section.

Plausible (`IMPORT-009` and `IMPORT-007`, optional `seed.json` external `plausible`) is read only on the owner’s request at the fixed official `https://plausible.io/api/v1/sites/goals`, `/api/v1/sites/custom-props` and `/api/v2/query` endpoints, without redirects. Requests send the source site ID, one-use Stats or Enterprise Sites API key and definition or aggregate queries; responses return goals, property names, aggregate revenue currencies or 14 complete overlap days of visitors, visits, pageviews, bounce rate, sources and goal counts for the owner-only parallel-run report. The comparison sends no native Vane rows, is transient and keeps neither raw source responses nor owner evidence in custody. No collected Vane visitor rows are sent. Only sanitized definition summaries enter reviewed import custody; the key and raw response never enter previews, journals, logs or archives. Dashboard ZIP import is local and carries no key.
