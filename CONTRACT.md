# Vane contract

Target edition: 17 October 2026. Vane is complete privacy-focused website analytics for a US small or midsize business that wants to replace Plausible Analytics, Fathom Analytics or Matomo Cloud, with the depth buyers now expect from Umami, Simple Analytics and PostHog Web Analytics. It counts cookie-free unique visitors, visits, bounce rate and visit duration. It shows real-time and historical traffic by page, source, campaign, location and device. It measures goals, funnels and revenue, and it reconciles them with real orders and leads. It sends scheduled reports and alerts and follows them up, and none of this needs a cookie banner. Agents keep the measurement honest: they explain why numbers moved, propose and ship changes under authority the owner grants, and measure the result.

This contract is the product Vane is building towards, not a description of today's code. The [Status](#status) section says, clause by clause, how close the code at the judged commit is. A gap in the code never narrows a clause. Every clause names the behaviour a buyer gets and the acceptance evidence, a journey or test through a public door, that proves it. ACCEPTANCE.md lists the same evidence for each build round. This documentation round changes no homepage, interface or runtime behaviour.

## Product target

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Category | Privacy-focused website analytics |
| Incumbents a buyer switches from | Plausible Analytics, Fathom Analytics, Matomo Cloud (and the up-and-comers Umami, Simple Analytics and PostHog Web Analytics) |
| First buyer | A US small or midsize business owner or marketing lead who runs their own websites (a services firm, a Shopify or WooCommerce store, an agency managing client sites), paying in USD and running Vane in their own Cloudflare account |

What switching means: the buyer installs one script, or a WordPress, Shopify, Webflow, Squarespace, Wix, Google Tag Manager or Pane path. Their Google Analytics 4, Plausible, Fathom, Matomo, Umami or Simple Analytics history comes across. On the first day they see the same visitors, visits, bounce rate, duration, sources, campaigns, locations, devices, goals, funnels and revenue the incumbent showed, under published definitions. Reports, alerts, shared dashboards, team access, the Stats API, Search Console keywords and a first-party proxy all work. No cookie banner is required for the default mode.

A buyer replacing an incumbent needs the whole job, so the target is the whole job:

- **Install and collect.** One script with live verification, platform install paths, SPA support, a server-side Events API, a first-party proxy, open path collection with redaction, and bot, spam and internal-traffic exclusion at scale (`INSTALL-*`, `COLLECT-*`, `COVERAGE-001`).
- **Measure people and engagement without cookies.** Daily-rotating salted hashes for unique visitors and visits, bounce, duration, scroll depth and a disclosed methodology. A consented mode adds returning visitors and retention (`METRIC-*`).
- **Understand traffic.** Real-time and historical dashboards; page, entry and exit, channel, referrer, UTM, location and device breakdowns; filters, segments, comparisons, annotations, journeys, site search, page performance and aggregate heatmaps (`DASH-*`, `PERF-001`, `HEAT-001`).
- **Measure conversions and revenue.** Goals, custom events with properties, automatic outbound-link, download, 404 and form tracking, funnels, revenue and ecommerce, conversion rates by source, experiments, and reconciliation with Stripe, Shopify, WooCommerce, Stall, HubSpot, Orbit, Pencil and Dial outcomes (`CONV-*`, `EXPERIMENT-001`).
- **Connect search and advertising.** Google Search Console and Bing Webmaster keywords, and ad spend from Google Ads, Microsoft Ads, Meta and LinkedIn matched to campaigns (`SEARCH-*`, `ADS-001`).
- **Move in.** GA4, Plausible, Fathom, Matomo, Umami, Simple Analytics and PostHog history, merged without double counting (`IMPORT-*`).
- **Report and follow up.** Scheduled email, Slack, Teams and Nook reports; spike, drop, conversion and tracking-health alerts; and follow-through that stays open until a person resolves it (`REPORT-*`, `ALERT-*`, `FOLLOW-001`).
- **Share and administer.** Shared, embedded and public dashboards; per-site roles, SSO and agency client workspaces; the Stats API, CSV and warehouse export, webhooks and MCP; privacy disclosure, consent managers and US state privacy signals; owner-run hosting with measured cost; and a phone-ready dashboard and native apps (`SHARE-*`, `TEAM-*`, `AGENCY-001`, `API-*`, `PRIVACY-*`, `HOST-001`, `MOBILE-*`).
- **Push further.** Agents keep the instrumentation correct and explain changes. They propose and ship site or campaign changes under granted authority, and they measure what happened against coverage gaps (`AGENT-*`).

### Neighbouring products stay separate

Vane measures traffic and outcomes. It links to the products that own the neighbouring jobs instead of rebuilding them:

- [Pane](https://github.com/just-every/run-pane) builds the website. Vane gives it a one-switch install path (`INSTALL-004`) and, under an owner's grant, drafts page changes there (`AGENT-003`).
- [Stall](https://github.com/just-every/run-stall) runs the storefront; Shopify, WooCommerce and Stripe are the external equivalents. Their orders confirm revenue conversions (`CONV-007`, `CONV-008`).
- [Orbit](https://github.com/just-every/run-orbit) (or HubSpot) is the CRM, [Pencil](https://github.com/just-every/run-pencil) the form builder and [Dial](https://github.com/just-every/run-dial) the booking tool. They confirm leads and bookings (`CONV-008`).
- [Mango](https://github.com/just-every/run-mango) sends email campaigns. Its sends become annotations and its links carry UTM tags (`DASH-009`).
- [Uptime](https://github.com/just-every/run-uptime) monitors availability and [Spool](https://github.com/just-every/run-spool) tracks errors. Their incidents become annotations and evidence in explanations (`DASH-009`, `AGENT-002`).
- [Crank](https://github.com/just-every/run-crank) (or Zapier and Make) automates downstream work from Vane webhooks (`API-003`), [Nook](https://github.com/just-every/run-nook) carries reports and alerts like Slack (`REPORT-002`), and [Auth](https://github.com/just-every/run-auth) can be the sign-in provider (`TEAM-002`).
- [Penny](https://github.com/just-every/run-penny) owns product feedback and roadmaps. Vane does not collect feedback.

## Existing guarantees

These clauses restate what the released first contract and ACCEPTANCE.md promised. They stay in force; where the target widens them, the target clause names which one it extends.

- **HTTP-001 — One Worker with honest public and private doors.** One Cloudflare Worker serves the installed instance. The Lobby is at `/`, with `/health`, `/signin` and `/setup`. The collection script is at `/vane.js`, with its collection and configuration endpoints. Private `/app` and `/api/vane/*` routes are behind native sessions, and unknown paths return real 404 responses. The public run site is a separate static surface. **Acceptance evidence:** Request `/health`, `/vane.js`, an unknown path and every private route signed out on a built Worker. Health answers, the script is served, unknown paths return 404, and private routes return 401 or redirect to sign-in (`tests/vane.test.mjs`).

- **ACCESS-001 — Owners approve, administrators prepare, members read.** Roles come from the native operator accounts. Members read sites and reports. Administrators also prepare site, settings and erasure proposals. Only the owner approves proposals, imports, restores and exports history. A revoked operator loses access on their next request. **Acceptance evidence:** Invite an administrator and a member. The member cannot prepare, the administrator cannot approve or import, export is owner-only, and revoking the administrator ends their session (`tests/vane.test.mjs`).

- **ACCESS-002 — Private doors refuse signed-out and cross-origin requests.** Every private read and mutation needs a native session, and every mutation needs the configured same origin. A direct transport call gets the same refusal as a browser route. **Acceptance evidence:** Call every private GET and POST door signed out, signed out after sign-out, and with a foreign Origin. Each is refused with no write (`tests/vane.test.mjs`).

- **APPROVE-001 — Prepared changes apply only on the owner's recorded approval.** A site, a settings change or a history erasure can be prepared in the UI or through the agent command API. Each change waits as a proposal until the owner approves it once through the approval form. The approver, the preparer and the time are recorded. Approval refuses a proposal whose site version has changed. The command API can prepare and read proposals but can never approve them. **Acceptance evidence:** Prepare as an administrator, approve as the owner, then approve again; the second approval gets 409. Inspect the proposal for distinct preparer and approver (`tests/vane.test.mjs`).

- **COLLECT-001 — A cookie-free script that sends only declared anonymous fields.** `vane.js` sets no cookie or browser storage. It sends no visitor or session identifier, user agent, raw URL or query values. It does nothing when Do Not Track or Global Privacy Control is set. Collection accepts events only from the site's registered origin and only with the declared field set. It refuses extra fields, such as an email address. The target field set and server-side derivations are widened by `COLLECT-002`, `METRIC-001` and `DASH-005`/`DASH-006` without ever adding a stored raw identifier. **Acceptance evidence:** Post an event from a foreign origin (403), with an extra personal field (400) and with a path carrying a query (400). A valid event is accepted (202) (`tests/vane.test.mjs`).

- **CONSENT-001 — The site's consent mode is enforced at collection.** A site chooses whether consent is required. When it is, only events that carry granted consent are admitted. Consent-mode changes go through `APPROVE-001`. **Acceptance evidence:** On a consent-required site, an event without granted consent is refused with 403 and one with granted consent is accepted (`tests/vane.test.mjs`).

- **DEDUP-001 — Each page load is admitted once.** A fresh UUID identifies each page load. A repeat with identical data is acknowledged as a duplicate without counting twice, and a repeat with different data is refused. **Acceptance evidence:** Send the same event twice (202, then 200 with nothing counted) and then a conflicting copy (409). The report counts one visit (`tests/vane.test.mjs`).

- **TRAFFIC-001 — Page loads, sources, campaigns and goals with a comparison and stated limits.** For any date range (default: the last seven days), the report shows accepted page loads, goal conversions, referring hostnames, campaigns, pages and goals. Each is compared with the preceding period of the same length. Beside them, the report states the limits of collection coverage: sampling, consent, configured paths, imports and unknown coverage. The page-load count stays visible as pageviews when `METRIC-001` and `METRIC-002` add visitors and visits. **Acceptance evidence:** Collect events in two adjacent periods and read the summary. Totals, breakdowns, the previous-period totals, the difference and the coverage-limit text match the seeded events.

- **IMPORT-001 — Reviewed, journalled and undoable Umami import.** The owner previews a documented Umami v2 Events page without any write. Visitor identifiers, titles and raw URLs are discarded before custody. The owner then approves the exact reviewed document by its fingerprint. The import is idempotent and resumable, every effect is journalled, and an undo removes its contributions while the journal remains. **Acceptance evidence:** Preview the import (no writes, no raw identifiers), apply it with a changed fingerprint (409), apply it twice (one job), read the journal and undo it (`tests/vane.test.mjs`).

- **ARCHIVE-001 — Complete history export and atomic restore.** The owner exports every analytics, proposal, deletion and import-custody table as an integrity-checked archive. Restore works only into an empty installation, keeps the owner sign-in, rejects a corrupt archive and preserves import undo. **Acceptance evidence:** Export, corrupt the archive and restore it (400, no writes). Restore the real archive into an empty installation, check that the tables are identical, restore it again (409) and undo the restored import (`tests/vane.test.mjs`).

- **ERASURE-001 — Owner-approved erasure, fenced against old backups.** An approved history erasure removes a site's live and imported events and retires its import custody. It records a deletion journal, and a later journal refuses the restore of an older archive that still holds the erased history. **Acceptance evidence:** Erase a site with live and imported history. Both are gone, the deletion and erasure journals exist, and restoring the old archive with the later journal is refused (`tests/vane.test.mjs`).

- **RETENTION-001 — The declared retention window erases older history.** Each site declares 30, 90 or 365 days of retention (the `PRIVACY-001` target adds 13 and 25 months and unlimited). Applying retention erases older live and imported events in resumable steps and records the deletion in the journal. **Acceptance evidence:** Import old history, collect a recent event and apply retention until it completes. The old history is gone, the recent event remains, and one deletion is journalled (`tests/vane.test.mjs`).

- **RUNSITE-001 — The public run site reaches installation and every guide.** The public homepage links to `/run` and `/docs`. `/run` shows the real `rer dev just-every/run-vane` launch command, and every guide's local links resolve. The run site contains only public routes, reads no installation data and loads only local assets; Run and Docs work without JavaScript. **Acceptance evidence:** `rer build --site`, then follow every local link from the built homepage and guides (`tests/site.test.mjs`).

- **DOCS-001 — Documentation built from repository Markdown.** `/docs` renders the repository's Markdown guides with readable code blocks, tables, section links and optional copy-to-clipboard controls. **Acceptance evidence:** Build the site and open each guide. Code blocks, tables and heading anchors render, and the clipboard control copies the block.

- **HOME-001 — The approved homepage in the shared theme.** The run site serves the owner-approved indigo-isobar landing page in the shared Geologica theme with locally served fonts. At phone width its three hero cards and feature bands stack. Its Fern figures are explanatory drawings and are never seeded as analytics. **Acceptance evidence:** The independent visual gate compares the built homepage at 1440 and 390 px with the approved specification and picks.

- **LOBBY-001 — The installed Lobby and real sign-in stay intact.** An installed instance keeps its Lobby at `/`, real sign-in and first-run setup flows, and its existing authenticated routes, all in the shared Vane theme. **Acceptance evidence:** On a fresh installation, bootstrap the owner, complete `/setup`, sign in by email link and open `/app`. No landing-drawing numbers appear in analytics.

- **DEPLOY-001 — Run-site deployment stays behind its switch.** Run-site deployment runs only when the owner sets `RUN_SITE_DEPLOY`. The run site is configured for run-vane.com. Existing edition, package, Worker and database identifiers are preserved. **Acceptance evidence:** With the variable unset, the run-site workflow skips deployment. The identifiers in `wrangler.jsonc`, `package.json` and `edition.json` are unchanged.

## Target clauses

### Install and collection

- **INSTALL-001 — One script tag with live install verification.** Settings show the snippet with a copy button. A **Verify installation** action loads the site's homepage server-side, finds the script and waits up to 60 seconds for a first event from that page. It then reports the exact cause of any failure: script missing, wrong site ID, origin mismatch, Content Security Policy blocking, consent flag never set, path excluded, or a blocker suspected. Until a first event arrives, the site shows **Awaiting first event** in the overview. **Acceptance evidence:** Point a fixture site at Vane without the script, then with the wrong site ID, then with a CSP that blocks it, then correctly. Each check names its cause; the correct one turns verified when the first event arrives.

- **INSTALL-002 — WordPress and WooCommerce plugin.** A WordPress.org plugin connects a WordPress site to a Vane installation with a site token. It inserts the script (or the proxied script from `INSTALL-006`), excludes logged-in administrators if the owner chooses, and turns on outbound-link, download, 404 and form events (`CONV-003`). With WooCommerce it records product views, add to cart, checkout and purchases with revenue (`CONV-007`). It shows a Vane dashboard widget in wp-admin. **Acceptance evidence:** Install the plugin on a WordPress + WooCommerce test site, connect it and place a test order. Pageviews, automatic events and a purchase with the correct USD revenue appear in Vane, and admin visits are excluded when that option is set.

- **INSTALL-003 — Shopify app with checkout tracking.** A Shopify app installs the script on the storefront through a theme app extension. It registers a Customer Events web pixel so that checkout steps and purchases (order value, currency, product lines) arrive as events with no customer personal data. Orders confirmed through the Shopify Admin API reconcile the purchases (`CONV-008`). **Acceptance evidence:** Install the app on a Shopify development store and complete a test checkout. Storefront pageviews, the checkout funnel steps and a reconciled purchase with its revenue appear.

- **INSTALL-004 — Webflow, Squarespace, Wix, Pane and static-site paths.** Step-by-step install guides with a verification check (`INSTALL-001`) cover Webflow (site-wide custom code), Squarespace (code injection), Wix (custom code or Velo) and common static hosts (Netlify, Vercel, GitHub Pages). Each guide also shows how to send the platform's form submissions as events. A [Pane](https://github.com/just-every/run-pane) site turns Vane on with one switch, and Pane's editor shows that page's visitors. **Acceptance evidence:** Follow each guide against its platform's test site and pass verification. Turn on Vane in a Pane site and see the page's visitors in the Pane editor.

- **INSTALL-005 — Google Tag Manager template.** A published GTM Community Template installs Vane, passes GTM consent state into `PRIVACY-003`, and maps dataLayer events to Vane custom events with declared properties. **Acceptance evidence:** Import the template into a GTM container, publish it and fire a dataLayer purchase. A Vane event with the mapped properties arrives, and denied consent sends nothing in a consent-required mode.

- **INSTALL-006 — First-party proxy so blockers do not erase data.** The owner can serve the script and collection endpoint from their own domain. Ready-made setups cover a Cloudflare Worker route (one click when the site's zone is in the same Cloudflare account), Next.js rewrites, Vercel, Netlify, Nginx, Apache and WordPress (`INSTALL-002`). A custom collection subdomain such as `stats.example.com` is also supported. The proxied path forwards the visitor's country and user agent for server-side derivation and never logs them. Reports label how much traffic arrived through the proxy (`COVERAGE-001`). **Acceptance evidence:** Configure the Cloudflare route and the Next.js rewrite and load the site with a common blocker list active. Events still arrive, the proxy share is reported, and the forwarded values are never stored raw.

- **INSTALL-007 — Single-page applications and hash routing.** The script records a pageview on every History API navigation and, when the owner turns it on, on hash changes. It also sends pageviews manually via `vane('pageview', {url})` for frameworks that control routing. **Acceptance evidence:** Navigate a React Router and a hash-routed fixture through four views. Four pageviews arrive with the right paths and no duplicates.

- **INSTALL-008 — Server-side and mobile Events API.** An authenticated `POST /api/v1/events` accepts pageviews and custom events from servers, backends and mobile apps. It takes the same fields and properties as the script, plus a client IP and user agent supplied by the caller, which are used only for `METRIC-001` hashing and `DASH-005`/`DASH-006` derivation and then discarded. Tokens are scoped to one site, with rate limits and idempotency keys. **Acceptance evidence:** Send events from a server with a site token, with a duplicate idempotency key and with a token for another site. Events count once, the foreign token is refused, and no raw IP or user agent is stored.

- **COLLECT-002 — Open path collection with automatic redaction.** Every page on a registered site is collected without a path allowlist. The current allowlist becomes an optional include or exclude list. Query strings are dropped except UTM parameters and owner-allowlisted keys, such as a site-search parameter (`DASH-012`). Ad click identifiers (gclid, fbclid, msclkid) are recorded only as a paid-click channel flag, never as values. Path segments that look like email addresses, long tokens, UUIDs or long numbers are replaced with placeholders before storage. Owners can add rewrite rules such as `/orders/*` → `/orders/:id`. Page titles are never stored. **Acceptance evidence:** Load pages with emails, tokens, order numbers and UTM and click-ID queries in the URL. Stored paths are redacted, UTM values and the paid-click flag are kept, and a rewrite rule groups the order pages.

- **COLLECT-003 — Bot, referrer-spam and internal-traffic exclusion.** At ingestion Vane drops known crawlers and automation, using the Cloudflare bot score where available plus a maintained user-agent list, data-centre traffic heuristics and a maintained referrer-spam list. Owners can exclude IP addresses or ranges (matched in memory and never stored), exclude pages, and exclude their own browser with a per-browser opt-out setting on the site. Excluded counts are reported by reason, never silently. **Acceptance evidence:** Send traffic from a headless browser, a spam referrer, an excluded IP and an opted-out browser beside real visits. Only the real visits count, and the exclusion report shows each reason and its count.

- **COLLECT-004 — Collection at small-business scale.** One installation collects at least 10 million pageviews a month across its sites with bursts of 500 events per second. Dashboards answer a 12-month query in under 2 seconds at that volume, using aggregated rollups and a declared analytical store (Cloudflare Analytics Engine, D1 rollups or the buyer's warehouse through `API-002`). Any limit is published and shown before it is reached: the current fixed 10,000-events-per-site-per-day admission ceiling is lifted. **Acceptance evidence:** A load test at the stated volume and burst rate admits every valid event exactly once. The 12-month dashboard query is timed, and the limit warning appears at the published threshold.

- **COVERAGE-001 — Coverage shown beside every number.** Every report states what it cannot see: the consent-declined share (the script sends a field-free declined counter when consent is not granted, if the owner enables it), sampling, exclusions by reason, the proxy and direct share, imported periods with unknown coverage, and deleted history. Where the site's Cloudflare zone is connected, Vane also compares its pageviews with zone page requests to estimate the share lost to blockers. No total is presented as complete when coverage is unknown. **Acceptance evidence:** Combine declined consent, sampling, exclusions, an import and a zone comparison on one site. The report shows each share with its denominator and labels the totals as partial.

### Visitors and engagement

- **METRIC-001 — Cookie-free unique visitors from daily-rotating salted hashes.** The server derives a visitor hash from SHA-256(daily salt + site ID + IP address + user agent). The salt is random, rotates at midnight in the site's time zone and is deleted after 24 hours. The IP and user agent are never stored, logged or returned. Unique visitors count distinct hashes within a day. For a multi-day range, the count is the sum of daily uniques unless consented mode (`METRIC-005`) applies, and the methodology page says so. A visitor cannot be followed across days or across sites. **Acceptance evidence:** Send pageviews from two simulated visitors over two days. Unique visitors are 2 per day and the range shows 4 under the stated method. Show that the database, logs and archive hold no IP, no user agent and no salt older than 24 hours, and that the same visitor hashes differently on another site.

- **METRIC-002 — Visits and pages per visit.** A visit is a run of events from one visitor hash with no gap over 30 minutes; it also ends when the daily salt rotates. Reports show visits, pageviews, pages per visit and views per visitor. **Acceptance evidence:** Send one visitor's pageviews with gaps of 10, 40 and 5 minutes. The report shows 2 visits, 4 pageviews and 2 pages per visit.

- **METRIC-003 — Bounce rate and visit duration.** A bounce is a visit with one pageview and no engagement event. Visit duration runs from the first event to the last engagement signal. The script sends a field-free engagement ping when the page is hidden or after 10 seconds or more of visible time, which counts time on the final page. Reports show the bounce rate and the median and average visit duration, per page as well as overall. **Acceptance evidence:** Simulate a quick bounce, a single-page visit with 45 seconds of engagement, and a three-page visit. The bounce rate, visit durations and per-page time match the definitions.

- **METRIC-004 — Methodology disclosed and versioned.** Every installation serves a public methodology page. It defines visitors, visits, bounce, duration, scroll depth, conversions and revenue, the hash recipe and salt lifetime, consented mode, sampling and estimation limits. Each metric in the app links to its definition. A change to a definition is versioned and appears as an annotation on every chart it affects (`DASH-009`). **Acceptance evidence:** Open the methodology page signed out and follow each metric's definition link from the dashboard. Change the visit timeout and see a definition annotation dated on the charts.

- **METRIC-005 — Consented visitor mode: returning visitors and retention.** A site can turn on a consented mode for visitors who grant analytics consent. It stores a random first-party identifier with a lifetime the owner chooses (at most 13 months) and adds new and returning visitors, multi-day unique visitors, multi-visit attribution and weekly retention cohorts, as in Matomo. Visitors without consent stay in cookie-free mode, and reports show the consented share. Revoking consent deletes the identifier on the device and stops linking. **Acceptance evidence:** With consent granted, visit on day 1 and day 8 and see one returning visitor in the week-2 cohort. Without consent, the same visits count as cookie-free daily visitors. A revoke deletes the identifier.

- **METRIC-006 — Scroll depth and time on page.** The script reports the maximum scroll depth reached on each page in 25% buckets, as part of the engagement ping. Page reports show the average scroll depth, the share reaching 75%, and the time on page. **Acceptance evidence:** Scroll a long fixture page to 50% and to 100% in separate visits. The page shows the average depth and the 75% share as expected.

### Dashboards and analysis

- **DASH-001 — Real-time dashboard.** A real-time view shows current visitors (active in the last 5 minutes), pageviews per minute for the last 30 minutes, and live top pages, sources and locations, refreshing at least every 15 seconds without a reload. **Acceptance evidence:** Send events while the real-time view is open. Current visitors and the live lists update within 15 seconds and drop once the visitors go idle for 5 minutes.

- **DASH-002 — Historical time series in the site's time zone.** The main chart plots visitors, visits, pageviews, bounce rate, duration, conversions and revenue by hour, day, week or month. Preset ranges cover today, yesterday, the last 7, 30 and 90 days, month to date, last month, year to date, the last 12 months and all time, and any custom range can be chosen. Days start at midnight in the site's time zone, which defaults to the owner's US time zone and handles daylight-saving changes. Every chart has an accessible data table. **Acceptance evidence:** Seed events either side of a US daylight-saving change and midnight in America/Chicago. The daily buckets follow local days, each preset range returns the expected totals, and the data table matches the chart.

- **DASH-003 — Page, entry and exit reports.** Page reports list top pages, entry (landing) pages and exit pages, each with visitors, pageviews, bounce rate, time on page, scroll depth and conversion rate. Pages can be grouped by path prefix or by a rewrite rule. **Acceptance evidence:** Seed visits with known entry and exit pages. The three tables match, and grouping by `/blog/*` sums the posts correctly.

- **DASH-004 — Channels, referrers and UTM campaigns.** Every visit gets a channel, using published definitions: Direct, Organic Search, Paid Search, Organic Social, Paid Social, Email, Referral, AI Assistants (ChatGPT, Perplexity, Claude, Gemini, Copilot), Affiliates and Display. Referrer domains and URLs come from public referring pages. All five UTM fields (source, medium, campaign, term, content) are available as dimensions. No allowlist is needed. Email-client and webmail referrers are grouped. **Acceptance evidence:** Send visits with Google organic, Google paid (gclid flag), Facebook, an email UTM, a chatgpt.com referrer and five-field UTMs. Each lands in its documented channel and every UTM field is reportable.

- **DASH-005 — Country, region and city.** Location comes from Cloudflare's request geolocation at ingestion: country, region (US state), city and US metro area (DMA), stored as names and never as an IP address. Very small city counts can be grouped on shared dashboards. **Acceptance evidence:** Send events with Cloudflare geo for Texas, Ontario and London. Reports show the country, region and city, and no IP appears anywhere in storage.

- **DASH-006 — Devices, browsers and operating systems.** The server derives the device class (desktop, mobile, tablet), browser and major version, operating system and version, and a screen-size class from the user agent and an optional viewport width. The raw user agent is discarded. **Acceptance evidence:** Send events from iPhone Safari, Android Chrome, Windows Edge and an iPad. The device, browser and OS tables match, and no raw user agent is stored.

- **DASH-007 — Filters, segments and saved views.** Clicking any dimension value filters the whole dashboard. Filters combine with is, is not, contains, does not contain and matches (regex), on any dimension or event property. Filter combinations can be saved as named segments, shared with the team, and used in reports, alerts and the API. **Acceptance evidence:** Filter by Organic Search and mobile and not `/blog/*`, save the segment and reopen it in a report and an alert. Each total equals a hand-computed count of the seeded events.

- **DASH-008 — Period comparisons.** Any range can be compared with the previous period, the same period last year or a custom period. Both series appear on the chart, and every table shows the change with its absolute and percentage values. Comparisons match weekdays when the owner chooses. **Acceptance evidence:** Compare the last 30 days with the previous period and with last year. The chart overlays and the per-row changes match the seeded data.

- **DASH-009 — Annotations, manual and automatic.** Team members add dated notes to charts. Vane adds annotations for settings and definition changes, imports, agent-shipped changes (`AGENT-003`), Mango campaign sends, Uptime incidents, Spool error spikes, deploy webhooks and GitHub releases. Annotations show on every affected chart, in reports and in agent explanations. **Acceptance evidence:** Add a manual note, approve a settings change, post a deploy webhook and receive an Uptime incident. All four annotations appear on the chart at the right time.

- **DASH-010 — Multi-site overview and roll-up.** The sites list shows each site's visitors, change and a sparkline for the selected range, and the list can be sorted and searched. An owner can create a roll-up view that combines several sites (for example, the marketing site and the store) with de-duplicated breakdowns. **Acceptance evidence:** With three sites, open the overview, sort it by change and create a roll-up of two of them. The roll-up totals equal the sum of the two sites.

- **DASH-011 — Journeys and paths.** From any page or entry page, a journey view shows the next and previous three steps within visits, with counts and drop-off, and it can be filtered by segment. **Acceptance evidence:** Seed visits through home → pricing → signup and home → blog → exit. The journey from home shows both paths with the right counts.

- **DASH-012 — Site-search analytics.** When the owner names the site-search query parameter, Vane reports search terms, searches per visit, the pages searches start from and the share of visits that exit after searching. A search term is shown only after at least two different visitors have searched it, so one person's search cannot be singled out. **Acceptance evidence:** Search `?q=returns` from two visitors and `?q=alice@example.com` from one. The returns term appears and the email-like term never does.

- **PERF-001 — Page performance.** The script can report Core Web Vitals (LCP, INP, CLS) and page load time, without identifiers, and Vane shows them per page and device class at the 75th percentile. Performance alerting and synthetic checks belong to [Uptime](https://github.com/just-every/run-uptime), which can read these figures. **Acceptance evidence:** Load a fixture page with an artificially slow image and see its LCP p75 above 2.5 s on that page only.

- **HEAT-001 — Aggregate click and scroll maps.** For pages the owner chooses, Vane records click positions on a grid and scroll depth, without identifiers, form contents or text. It shows them as a heatmap and a scroll map over a live screenshot of the page. No individual session can be replayed. **Acceptance evidence:** Click two buttons repeatedly on a fixture page. The heatmap concentrates on them, no event holds text or identifiers, and the scroll map matches `METRIC-006`.

### Conversions and revenue

- **CONV-001 — Pageview goals with patterns and conversion rates.** A goal can be a single path or a wildcard pattern (`/thank-you*`, `/blog/**`). Reports show conversions, unique converting visitors and the conversion rate against visitors. **Acceptance evidence:** Define `/thanks*` and visit `/thanks` and `/thanks/pro`. Both convert, and the conversion rate equals converting visitors divided by visitors.

- **CONV-002 — Custom events with properties.** Sites send events through `vane('Signup', {props: {plan: 'pro'}})` or by adding CSS classes (`vane-event-name=Signup`, with property classes). An event can carry up to 30 properties with string or number values. Property keys and values pass the `COLLECT-002` redaction, and an event carrying an email address is refused. Properties are dimensions for filtering, breakdowns and funnels. **Acceptance evidence:** Fire Signup with plan=pro and plan=free through JavaScript and CSS classes. The breakdown by plan matches, and a property holding an email address is refused.

- **CONV-003 — Automatic outbound-link, file-download, 404 and form-submission events.** Each automatic event is turned on per site. Outbound links record the destination URL without its query, downloads record the file path for common document and media extensions, 404 pages record the missing path (the site marks its 404 page), and form submissions record the form's ID or action path, never field values. **Acceptance evidence:** Click an outbound link and a PDF link, open a 404 page and submit a form on a fixture site. Four events arrive with the right properties and no form contents.

- **CONV-004 — Conversion rates by source, campaign, landing page and audience.** Every breakdown (channel, referrer, UTM, entry page, country, device) can show the conversions, conversion rate and revenue for a chosen goal. **Acceptance evidence:** Seed conversions from Google and from email. The channel table shows each channel's conversion rate and revenue for the selected goal.

- **CONV-005 — Funnels.** An owner or analyst defines a funnel of 2 to 8 steps (pageviews, wildcard paths or custom events with property conditions), ordered within one visit, or within one visitor across visits in consented mode. The funnel report shows the entrants, conversion and drop-off at each step, the median time between steps and a segment filter. Clicking a drop-off opens the journeys (`DASH-011`). **Acceptance evidence:** Define the funnel product → cart → checkout → purchase and seed visits that drop at each step. The step counts and rates match, and the segment filter recomputes them.

- **CONV-006 — Revenue goals.** A custom event can carry a revenue amount and an ISO currency. USD is the default reporting currency, and other currencies are converted at the day's stored exchange rate, which is shown. Reports show total revenue, average order value, and revenue per visitor by source, campaign, page and segment. Payment-processor referrers (Stripe Checkout, PayPal, Shop Pay, Klarna, Afterpay) are excluded from attribution, so the original source keeps credit. **Acceptance evidence:** Record USD and CAD purchases with a Stripe Checkout return referrer. Revenue totals in USD with the stated rate, and attribution goes to the original source rather than Stripe.

- **CONV-007 — Ecommerce events.** Standard events cover product view, add to cart, begin checkout and purchase, with a product SKU, name, category, quantity and price. WooCommerce (`INSTALL-002`), Shopify (`INSTALL-003`) and [Stall](https://github.com/just-every/run-stall) emit them automatically, and a ready-made ecommerce funnel and a product-performance report come with them. **Acceptance evidence:** Run a test purchase through each of the three platforms. The ecommerce funnel and the product report show the product, quantity and revenue.

- **CONV-008 — Conversions reconciled with real orders and leads.** Connected outcome sources confirm browser conversions by an opaque order or submission ID, never by customer details. Stripe confirms through webhooks, Shopify and WooCommerce through their order APIs, and Stall through its events. Lead and booking sources are HubSpot forms and CRM, [Orbit](https://github.com/just-every/run-orbit), [Pencil](https://github.com/just-every/run-pencil) and [Dial](https://github.com/just-every/run-dial). Reports show browser-reported, confirmed, unconfirmed and server-only outcomes side by side, with refunds subtracted, and attribute a server-only outcome to a source only when its browser event matched. **Acceptance evidence:** Complete three Stripe test payments, one with the browser event blocked and one refunded, and two HubSpot form submissions, one later marked qualified. The report shows each outcome in its correct confirmed or unconfirmed column, with net revenue.

- **CONV-009 — No-code event definitions.** In the app, an owner or analyst can define events without editing the site: a click on a CSS selector, a form submission, an element becoming visible, or a pageview pattern. A definition is previewed against the live page and applied through `APPROVE-001`, and the script picks it up from the site configuration. **Acceptance evidence:** Define a click event for `#book-demo`, preview the matched element and approve it. Clicks on the live fixture arrive as the event, with no site code change.

- **EXPERIMENT-001 — A/B tests with honest results.** An owner sets up a split test of 2 to 4 variants on a page. Assignment is stable within a visitor's day in cookie-free mode, and across days in consented mode. The site applies a variant through the script API, or through Pane or the buyer's code. Results show each variant's visitors, conversions and conversion rate with a 95% interval, the probability that it beats control, a minimum-sample warning, and a sample-ratio-mismatch check. A test cannot be called while it is underpowered. **Acceptance evidence:** Run a fixture test with a seeded 20% lift and a null test. The first reaches significance with the expected interval, the null test never does, and a skewed split triggers the mismatch warning.

### Reports, alerts and follow-up

- **REPORT-001 — Scheduled email reports.** Each team member, or an external recipient the owner adds, can subscribe to daily, weekly or monthly email reports for chosen sites and segments. Reports are sent at a chosen local time in the site's time zone. Each one shows the headline metrics with their comparison, top pages, sources, goals and revenue, the coverage notes and the explanation (`REPORT-003`). Every email has a one-click unsubscribe. Delivery failures show in the app and retry within the mail provider's rules. **Acceptance evidence:** Subscribe two recipients to the weekly report at 8:00 America/New_York and run the schedule across a daylight-saving change. Each email arrives once at the local hour with figures that match the dashboard, unsubscribe stops it, and a bounced delivery shows in the app.

- **REPORT-002 — Slack, Microsoft Teams and Nook delivery.** Reports and alerts can be sent to a Slack channel (Slack app with OAuth), a Microsoft Teams channel (Teams app or workflow webhook) or a [Nook](https://github.com/just-every/run-nook) channel, with a link back to the filtered dashboard. **Acceptance evidence:** Against fixture endpoints for all three, send a weekly report and an alert. Each payload carries the right figures and a working deep link, and a revoked token surfaces as a delivery failure.

- **REPORT-003 — A readable explanation of what changed.** Every report and alert explains its change by its largest contributors: which channels, pages, campaigns, locations or devices moved, and by how much, with denominators. It includes annotations in the window and any coverage change, and it says when the change is within normal variation. The explanation never claims a cause. **Acceptance evidence:** Seed a week where organic search falls 40% on one page while other channels are stable. The explanation names that page and channel with its denominators, lists the overlapping annotation, and labels a small fluctuation as normal variation.

- **ALERT-001 — Traffic spike and drop alerts.** An owner sets an alert on visitors, visits or pageviews for a site or segment. It fires on a fixed threshold, or on an anomaly against a day-of-week and hour baseline that accounts for US federal holidays and the owner's business calendar. Alerts have quiet hours, a cool-down and a recipients list across email, Slack, Teams, Nook and webhook. **Acceptance evidence:** Seed eight weeks of baseline traffic, then inject a 4× spike, a 70% drop and a Thanksgiving-day dip. The spike and drop alert once each, the holiday dip does not, and the cool-down suppresses a repeat.

- **ALERT-002 — Conversion, revenue and tracking-health alerts.** Alerts also cover a goal or revenue drop and a conversion-rate change. Tracking-health alerts cover a site with prior traffic that goes silent, a goal that stops firing after a deploy, a surge in collection errors, a collapse in the consent rate, and a divergence between browser-reported and confirmed outcomes (`CONV-008`). **Acceptance evidence:** Remove the script from a fixture site, break a goal's event name and block browser purchase events while Stripe confirms orders. Each condition raises its own named alert.

- **FOLLOW-001 — Alerts are followed through until a person resolves them.** Each alert opens an item with a responsible person, a next due action and a due time in local business hours. The item can be acknowledged, assigned, commented on and resolved with an outcome: real change, tracking fault fixed, expected, or ignored. Unacknowledged items remind the responsible person, then escalate to a second recipient, on the owner's schedule. Reminders stop when the item is resolved, the metric recovers and a person confirms it, or the owner ends the policy. A delivery or connector failure keeps the item open, and an alert notification alone never closes it. **Acceptance evidence:** Fire a drop alert, leave it unacknowledged past the reminder time, then the escalation time. One reminder and one escalation arrive. Acknowledge and resolve it as a tracking fault. Reminders stop and the item records who resolved it, when and with what outcome.

## Push further with agents

These agents work on the site's own analytics, its connected outcome sources and the grants the owner gives. They go beyond chat over charts: they keep the measurement trustworthy, act within granted authority and measure what happened. Every agent action is audited (`AUDIT-001`), and every change an agent ships is annotated (`DASH-009`) and can be reversed. No agent may add an identifier, bypass consent, change a metric definition silently, invent revenue, or match identities across sites.

- **AGENT-001 — Maintain trustworthy instrumentation.** The agent compares the declared goals, events and funnels with what actually arrives. It also reads the site's pages (by crawling the sitemap), deploy diffs from a connected GitHub repository or Pane, the consent state, and server-side counts from Stripe, Shopify or the CRM (`CONV-008`). It finds missing, duplicated, misnamed and newly broken events, pages without the script, and funnels whose steps no longer exist. For each problem it drafts a fix: a pull request to the site repository, a GTM template change, a Pane setting or a no-code event (`CONV-009`). After the fix ships it verifies the real payload. A human approves each fix, and the agent never widens the collected fields. **Acceptance evidence:** Deploy a fixture site change that renames the signup button and drops the script from one template. The agent reports both with evidence and drafts a PR and a no-code event. After the owner approves and the change deploys, the agent confirms the events arrive again and annotates the fix.

- **AGENT-002 — Explain why traffic or conversions moved.** When numbers change, the agent separates four causes. Tracking coverage covers event volume against confirmed outcomes, consent rate, script errors and blockers. Channel mix covers the contributions by source, campaign and search query (`SEARCH-001`). Page behaviour covers funnel steps, page speed (`PERF-001`) and errors from Spool. External events cover Uptime incidents, Mango sends, ad-spend changes, seasonality and US holidays. It returns ranked hypotheses, each with denominators, linked evidence, its confidence and the observation that would disprove it. Missing coverage is stated as missing, never as a cause, and the agent never claims causality from a correlation. **Acceptance evidence:** Seed three fixture weeks: a tracking break, an organic ranking drop, and a real checkout failure that confirmed orders corroborate. For each, the agent ranks the true cause first with cited evidence and names what is unknown.

- **AGENT-003 — Propose and ship a site or campaign change under granted authority.** From an explanation or an opportunity (`AGENT-006`), the agent drafts a concrete change. The change can be page copy, a call to action, a title and meta description, a broken link or a form fix, a UTM correction, or a campaign budget or schedule change. It states the expected effect, its guardrail metrics and how it will be measured (`AGENT-004`). The agent ships only through grants the owner has given: a Pane page edit, a pull request to the site's GitHub repository, a Webflow CMS item, a Mango campaign draft, or an ad-platform change. Spend changes and material copy always need a person's approval, and every grant can be revoked. **Acceptance evidence:** Grant Pane page-edit and GitHub PR access but no ad access. The agent ships an approved CTA change in Pane and opens a PR for a title change. Its proposed budget change waits for approval and cannot be shipped without the ad grant, and revoking the Pane grant blocks the next edit.

- **AGENT-004 — Measure the result against coverage gaps.** For every shipped change, the agent runs an experiment (`EXPERIMENT-001`) where it can, or a before-and-after comparison with matched weekdays and guardrails where it cannot. It reports the measured effect with an interval, whether coverage changed in the window, and a verdict: measured improvement, no detectable change, worse, or inconclusive because of coverage or sample size. It recommends keeping or reverting the change, and it reverts only under the grant, with approval. **Acceptance evidence:** Ship two fixture changes: one with a real lift under an experiment, and one before-and-after change during a consent-rate drop. The first is reported as a measured improvement with its interval, the second as inconclusive because of coverage, and an approved revert restores the prior page.

- **AGENT-005 — Pursue anomalies to resolution.** On an alert (`ALERT-001`, `ALERT-002`), the agent investigates with `AGENT-002` first and attaches its findings to the follow-up item (`FOLLOW-001`). It contacts the responsible person only when a decision or an outside fix is needed, for example a developer for a broken event or the marketer for a paused campaign, on the owner's channels and schedule. It keeps the item open until it is resolved, and it closes it only on a person's confirmation or a verified recovery with coverage intact. **Acceptance evidence:** Fire a silent-site alert caused by a deploy. The agent attaches its evidence, messages the configured developer once, reminds them after the due time, and after the fix verifies events and closes the item with that evidence. A real traffic dip with no fault is closed as expected after the owner confirms.

- **AGENT-006 — Weekly growth brief with opportunities.** Each week the agent writes a brief of three to five opportunities, each with figures. Examples are pages with high entry and high exit, campaigns that convert above the site's average, Search Console queries ranking 4 to 20 with high impressions, slow pages that convert below their peers, and stalled funnel steps. It links each one to a drafted `AGENT-003` change. The owner can dismiss an opportunity type, and the agent stops raising it. **Acceptance evidence:** On a fixture site with seeded opportunities, the brief names each with correct figures and a linked draft. A dismissed type does not return the next week.

- **AGENT-007 — Answer questions in plain English, with the query shown.** In the app, Slack, Teams and MCP, a team member asks a question such as "Which campaigns brought paying customers from Texas last month?". The answer comes back as a chart, the figures, and the exact filters, metrics and range used. The figures equal what the dashboard shows for that query. Ambiguous questions get a clarifying choice, and questions the data cannot answer (people, causes, data outside coverage) say so. **Acceptance evidence:** Ask ten fixture questions, including an ambiguous one and an unanswerable one. Every numeric answer equals the Stats API result for the shown query, the ambiguous question gets a choice and the unanswerable one says why.

## Honest boundaries

- **No identity graph, fingerprinting or ad-exchange attribution.** Vane measures first-party traffic under declared modes. It never builds cross-site identities, fingerprints or ad audiences, and it does not place ad pixels. Buyers who need platform conversion tracking keep the ad platform's own tag, through GTM. Vane reads campaign spend under `ADS-001` and changes campaigns only under an `AGENT-003` grant.
- **"Cookieless" is not a legal guarantee.** Vane shows its exact fields, identifier lifetimes and consent behaviour (`PRIVACY-001`) and supports consent managers (`PRIVACY-003`). The owner and their counsel choose the mode. Vane does not certify compliance with GDPR, ePrivacy, CCPA/CPRA or any other state law.
- **Counts are estimates with stated coverage.** Visitors are hashed daily estimates, not people. Revenue and leads are confirmed only through the connected Stripe, Shopify, WooCommerce, Stall, CRM and form sources (`CONV-008`), and causal lift is claimed only under an experiment (`EXPERIMENT-001`). Blocked and declined traffic is estimated and disclosed, never filled in.
- **No individual session replay.** Vane offers aggregate heatmaps, scroll maps, journeys and funnels. A buyer who needs per-visitor recordings uses PostHog, Microsoft Clarity or Hotjar under their own consent; Vane links to it from annotations and does not store recordings.
- **No logged-in product analytics with person profiles.** Website and app traffic through the Events API is in scope. Per-user product analytics, feature flags and person profiles stay in PostHog, Amplitude or Mixpanel. Vane's webhooks and warehouse export (`API-002`, `API-003`) feed them.
- **Tag management and SEO tooling are not rebuilt.** Third-party marketing tags stay in Google Tag Manager, which Vane installs through (`INSTALL-005`). Backlink, rank-tracking and site-audit suites (Ahrefs, Semrush) stay separate. Vane reads Search Console and Bing keyword data.
- **Neighbouring jobs stay with their products.** Website building is Pane, storefronts are Stall or Shopify, CRM is Orbit or HubSpot, forms are Pencil, bookings are Dial, email campaigns are Mango, uptime is Uptime, errors are Spool, feedback is Penny and automation is Crank. Vane integrates with each of them.
- **Raw event storage has a capacity design.** History is stored as the rollups and anonymous events declared in `COLLECT-004` within published limits. Unlimited raw history goes to the buyer's warehouse (`API-002`).
- **US first.** USD reporting, US time zones and daylight saving, US federal holidays in baselines, US state privacy signals and a US data location are the defaults. Other currencies, regions and the EU jurisdiction are supported, but not first.

## Declared data flows

Today Vane receives only page-load events from registered origins, sends only sign-in and invitation mail through the configured Base mail adapter, and declares Cloudflare as its only external in `seed.json`. Each target integration is a new outbound data flow. These are Google Search Console, Google Analytics, Bing, ad platforms, Stripe, Shopify, WooCommerce, HubSpot, Slack, Microsoft Teams, warehouses, consent managers, Pane, Stall, Orbit, Pencil, Dial, Mango, Uptime, Spool, Nook, Crank, GitHub, Webflow and MCP clients. Each one ships only with its `seed.json` externals entry, its scopes, the data it sends and receives, and token custody (encrypted at rest, revocable and excluded from archives) declared in this section.
