# Vane contract

Target edition: 17 October 2026. Vane is complete privacy-focused website analytics for a US small or midsize business that wants to replace Plausible Analytics, Fathom Analytics or Matomo Cloud, with the depth buyers now expect from Umami, Simple Analytics and PostHog Web Analytics. It counts cookie-free unique visitors, visits, bounce rate and visit duration. It shows real-time and historical traffic by page, source, campaign, location and device. It measures goals, funnels and revenue, and it reconciles them with real orders and leads. It sends scheduled reports and alerts and follows them up, and none of this needs a cookie banner. Agents keep the measurement honest: they explain why numbers moved, propose and ship changes under authority the owner grants, and measure the result.

This contract is the product Vane is building towards, not a description of today's code. The [Status](#status) section says, clause by clause, how close the code at the judged commit is. A gap in the code never narrows a clause. Every clause names the behaviour a buyer gets and the acceptance evidence, a journey or test through a public door, that proves it. ACCEPTANCE.md lists the same evidence for each build round. This documentation round changes no homepage, interface or runtime behaviour.

## Product target

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Category | Privacy-focused website analytics |
| Incumbents a buyer switches from | Plausible Analytics, Fathom Analytics, Matomo Cloud (and the up-and-comers Umami, Simple Analytics and PostHog Web Analytics) |
| First buyer | A US small or midsize business owner or marketing lead who runs their own websites (a services firm, a Shopify or WooCommerce store, an agency managing client sites), paying in USD and running Vane in their own Cloudflare account |

What switching means: the buyer installs one script, or a WordPress, Shopify, Webflow, Squarespace, Wix, Google Tag Manager or Pane path. Their Google Analytics 4, Plausible, Fathom, Matomo, Umami or Simple Analytics history comes across, their existing `plausible()` and `fathom.trackEvent()` calls keep counting, their goals and custom properties are recreated for review from the sources their plan exposes, and their funnels are rebuilt in a guided step. On the first day they see the same visitors, visits, bounce rate, duration, sources, campaigns, locations, devices, goals, funnels and revenue the incumbent showed, under published definitions. Reports, alerts, shared dashboards, team access, the Stats API, Search Console keywords and a first-party proxy all work. No cookie banner is required for the default mode.

A buyer replacing an incumbent needs the whole job, so the target is the whole job:

- **Install and collect.** One script with live verification, platform install paths, SPA support, a server-side Events API, a first-party proxy, open path collection with redaction, and bot, spam and internal-traffic exclusion at scale (`INSTALL-*`, `COLLECT-*`, `COVERAGE-001`).
- **Measure people and engagement without cookies.** Daily-rotating salted hashes for unique visitors and visits, bounce, duration, scroll depth and a disclosed methodology. A consented mode adds returning visitors and retention (`METRIC-*`).
- **Understand traffic.** Real-time and historical dashboards; page, entry and exit, channel, referrer, UTM, hostname, location and device breakdowns; filters, segments, comparisons, annotations, journeys, site search, page performance and aggregate heatmaps (`DASH-*`, `PERF-001`, `HEAT-001`).
- **Measure conversions and revenue.** Goals, custom events with properties, automatic outbound-link, download, 404 and form tracking, funnels, revenue and ecommerce, conversion rates by source, experiments, and reconciliation with Stripe, Shopify, WooCommerce, Stall, HubSpot, Orbit, Pencil and Dial outcomes (`CONV-*`, `EXPERIMENT-001`).
- **Connect search and advertising.** Google Search Console and Bing Webmaster keywords, and ad spend from Google Ads, Microsoft Ads, Meta and LinkedIn matched to campaigns (`SEARCH-*`, `ADS-001`).
- **Move in.** GA4, Plausible, Fathom, Matomo, Umami, Simple Analytics and PostHog history, merged without double counting. On cut-over day the incumbent's tracking calls keep counting, its goals and properties are recovered from what its plan exposes, and its funnels are rebuilt in a guided step (`IMPORT-*`).
- **Report and follow up.** Scheduled email, Slack, Teams and Nook reports; spike, drop, conversion and tracking-health alerts; and follow-through that stays open until a person resolves it (`REPORT-*`, `ALERT-*`, `FOLLOW-001`).
- **Share and administer.** Shared, embedded and public dashboards; per-site roles, SSO and agency client workspaces; the Stats API, CSV and warehouse export, webhooks and MCP; privacy disclosure, consent managers and US state privacy signals; owner-run hosting with measured cost; and a phone-ready dashboard and native apps (`SHARE-*`, `TEAM-*`, `AGENCY-001`, `API-*`, `PRIVACY-*`, `HOST-001`, `MOBILE-*`).
- **Push further.** Agents keep the instrumentation correct and explain changes. They propose and ship site or campaign changes under granted authority, and they measure what happened against coverage gaps (`AGENT-*`).

### Neighbouring products stay separate

Vane measures traffic and outcomes. It links to the products that own the neighbouring jobs instead of rebuilding them:

- [Pane](https://github.com/just-every/run-pane) builds the website. Vane gives it a one-switch install path (`INSTALL-004`) and, under an owner's grant, drafts page changes there (`AGENT-003`).
- [Stall](https://github.com/just-every/run-stall) runs the storefront; Shopify, WooCommerce and Stripe are the external equivalents. Their orders confirm revenue conversions (`CONV-007`, `CONV-008`).
- [Orbit](https://github.com/just-every/run-orbit) (or HubSpot) is the CRM, [Pencil](https://github.com/just-every/run-pencil) the form builder and [Dial](https://github.com/just-every/run-dial) the booking tool. They confirm leads and bookings (`CONV-008`).
- [Mango](https://github.com/just-every/run-mango) sends email campaigns. Its sends become annotations and its links carry UTM tags (`DASH-009`).
- [Uptime](https://github.com/just-every/run-uptime) monitors availability and [Spool](https://github.com/just-every/run-spool) tracks errors. Their incidents become annotations and evidence in explanations (`DASH-009`, `AGENT-002`).
- [Crank](https://github.com/just-every/run-crank) (or Zapier and Make) automates downstream work from Vane webhooks (`API-003`), [Nook](https://github.com/just-every/run-nook) carries reports and alerts like Slack (`REPORT-002`), and [Auth](https://github.com/just-every/run-auth) can be the sign-in provider (`TEAM-002`).
- [Penny](https://github.com/just-every/run-penny) owns product feedback and roadmaps. Vane does not collect feedback.

## Existing guarantees

These clauses restate what the released first contract and ACCEPTANCE.md promised. They stay in force; where the target widens them, the target clause names which one it extends.

- **HTTP-001 — One Worker with honest public and private doors.** One Cloudflare Worker serves the installed instance. The Lobby is at `/`, with `/health`, `/signin` and `/setup`. The collection script is at `/vane.js`, with its collection and configuration endpoints. Private `/app` and `/api/vane/*` routes are behind native sessions, and unknown paths return real 404 responses. The public run site is a separate static surface. **Acceptance evidence:** Request `/health`, `/vane.js`, an unknown path and every private route signed out on a built Worker. Health answers, the script is served, unknown paths return 404, and private routes return 401 or redirect to sign-in (`tests/vane.test.mjs`).

- **ACCESS-001 — Owners approve, administrators prepare, members read.** Roles come from the native operator accounts. Members read sites and reports. Administrators also prepare site, settings and erasure proposals. Only the owner approves proposals, imports, restores and exports history. A revoked operator loses access on their next request. **Acceptance evidence:** Invite an administrator and a member. The member cannot prepare, the administrator cannot approve or import, export is owner-only, and revoking the administrator ends their session (`tests/vane.test.mjs`).

- **ACCESS-002 — Private doors refuse signed-out and cross-origin requests.** Every private read and mutation needs a native session, and every mutation needs the configured same origin. A direct transport call gets the same refusal as a browser route. **Acceptance evidence:** Call every private GET and POST door signed out, signed out after sign-out, and with a foreign Origin. Each is refused with no write (`tests/vane.test.mjs`).

- **APPROVE-001 — Prepared changes apply only on the owner's recorded approval.** A site, a settings change or a history erasure can be prepared in the UI or through the agent command API. Each change waits as a proposal until the owner approves it once through the approval form. The approver, the preparer and the time are recorded. Approval refuses a proposal whose site version has changed. The command API can prepare and read proposals but can never approve them. **Acceptance evidence:** Prepare as an administrator, approve as the owner, then approve again; the second approval gets 409. Inspect the proposal for distinct preparer and approver (`tests/vane.test.mjs`).

- **COLLECT-001 — A cookie-free script that sends only declared anonymous fields.** `vane.js` sets no cookie or browser storage. It sends no visitor or session identifier, user agent, raw URL or query values. It does nothing when Do Not Track or Global Privacy Control is set. Collection accepts events only from the site's registered origin and only with the declared field set. It refuses extra fields, such as an email address. The target field set and server-side derivations are widened by `COLLECT-002`, `DASH-013`, `METRIC-001` and `DASH-005`/`DASH-006` without ever adding a stored raw identifier. The target allows exactly two declared exceptions to "no browser storage", and the methodology page (`METRIC-004`) and data inventory (`PRIVACY-001`) name both. One is the owner's own-browser exclusion (`COLLECT-003`): a single `vane_ignore` localStorage flag holding no identifier, written only when the owner opts that browser out. The other is the consented identifier (`METRIC-005`), written only after granted consent. A visitor in the default mode gets no cookie and no storage. **Acceptance evidence:** Post an event from a foreign origin (403), with an extra personal field (400) and with a path carrying a query (400). A valid event is accepted (202) (`tests/vane.test.mjs`). In a browser, a default-mode pageview leaves no cookie and no storage entry, and only the owner's opt-out writes `vane_ignore`.

- **CONSENT-001 — The site's consent mode is enforced at collection.** A site chooses whether consent is required. When it is, only events that carry granted consent are admitted. Consent-mode changes go through `APPROVE-001`. **Acceptance evidence:** On a consent-required site, an event without granted consent is refused with 403 and one with granted consent is accepted (`tests/vane.test.mjs`).

- **DEDUP-001 — Each page load is admitted once.** A fresh UUID identifies each page load. A repeat with identical data is acknowledged as a duplicate without counting twice, and a repeat with different data is refused. **Acceptance evidence:** Send the same event twice (202, then 200 with nothing counted) and then a conflicting copy (409). The report counts one visit (`tests/vane.test.mjs`).

- **TRAFFIC-001 — Page loads, sources, campaigns and goals with a comparison and stated limits.** For any date range (default: the last seven days), the report shows accepted page loads, goal conversions, referring hostnames, campaigns, pages and goals. Each is compared with the preceding period of the same length. Beside them, the report states the limits of collection coverage: sampling, consent, configured paths, imports and unknown coverage. The page-load count stays visible as pageviews when `METRIC-001` and `METRIC-002` add visitors and visits. **Acceptance evidence:** Collect events in two adjacent periods and read the summary. Totals, breakdowns, the previous-period totals, the difference and the coverage-limit text match the seeded events.

- **IMPORT-001 — Reviewed, journalled and undoable Umami import.** The owner previews a documented Umami v2 Events page without any write. Visitor identifiers, titles and raw URLs are discarded before custody. The owner then approves the exact reviewed document by its fingerprint. The import is idempotent and resumable, every effect is journalled, and an undo removes its contributions while the journal remains. **Acceptance evidence:** Preview the import (no writes, no raw identifiers), apply it with a changed fingerprint (409), apply it twice (one job), read the journal and undo it (`tests/vane.test.mjs`).

- **ARCHIVE-001 — Complete history export and atomic restore.** The owner exports every analytics, proposal, deletion and import-custody table as an integrity-checked archive. Restore works only into an empty installation, keeps the owner sign-in, rejects a corrupt archive and preserves import undo. **Acceptance evidence:** Export, corrupt the archive and restore it (400, no writes). Restore the real archive into an empty installation, check that the tables are identical, restore it again (409) and undo the restored import (`tests/vane.test.mjs`).

- **ERASURE-001 — Owner-approved erasure, fenced against old backups.** An approved history erasure removes a site's live and imported events and retires its import custody. It records a deletion journal, and a later journal refuses the restore of an older archive that still holds the erased history. **Acceptance evidence:** Erase a site with live and imported history. Both are gone, the deletion and erasure journals exist, and restoring the old archive with the later journal is refused (`tests/vane.test.mjs`).

- **RETENTION-001 — The declared retention window erases older history.** Each site declares 30, 90 or 365 days of retention (the `PRIVACY-001` target adds 13 and 25 months and unlimited). Applying retention erases older live and imported events in resumable steps and records the deletion in the journal. **Acceptance evidence:** Import old history, collect a recent event and apply retention until it completes. The old history is gone, the recent event remains, and one deletion is journalled (`tests/vane.test.mjs`).

- **RUNSITE-001 — The public run site reaches installation and every guide.** The public homepage links to `/run` and `/docs`. `/run` shows the real `rer dev just-every/run-vane` launch command, and every guide's local links resolve. The run site contains only public routes, reads no installation data and loads only local assets; Run and Docs work without JavaScript. **Acceptance evidence:** `rer build --site`, then follow every local link from the built homepage and guides (`tests/site.test.mjs`).

- **DOCS-001 — Documentation built from repository Markdown.** `/docs` renders the repository's Markdown guides with readable code blocks, tables, section links and optional copy-to-clipboard controls. **Acceptance evidence:** Build the site and open each guide. Code blocks, tables and heading anchors render, and the clipboard control copies the block.

- **HOME-001 — The approved homepage in the shared theme.** The run site serves the owner-approved design-desk landing page (17 Oct 2026: the weather vane in an open sky, the swallow mark, Inter Tight and Inter type) in the shared theme with locally served fonts and artwork. At phone width its hero cards and every section's drawing stack under their copy. Its Juniper Coffee figures are explanatory drawings and are never seeded as analytics. **Acceptance evidence:** The independent visual gate compares the built homepage at 1440 and 390 px with the approved specification and picks. In the repository, a browser opens the built homepage at 390 and 1440 px: at 390 px the hero cards and every section's drawing sit under their copy with no sideways scrolling, at 1440 px each drawing sits beside its copy, and every font, image and style comes from the run site itself, with Inter Tight and Inter loaded.

- **LOBBY-001 — The installed Lobby and real sign-in stay intact.** An installed instance keeps its Lobby at `/`, real sign-in and first-run setup flows, and its existing authenticated routes, all in the shared Vane theme. **Acceptance evidence:** On a fresh installation, bootstrap the owner, complete `/setup`, sign in by email link and open `/app`. No landing-drawing numbers appear in analytics.

- **DEPLOY-001 — Run-site deployment stays behind its switch.** Run-site deployment runs only when the owner sets `RUN_SITE_DEPLOY`. The run site is configured for run-vane.com. Existing edition, package, Worker and database identifiers are preserved. **Acceptance evidence:** With the variable unset, the run-site workflow skips deployment. The identifiers in `wrangler.jsonc`, `package.json` and `edition.json` are unchanged.

## Target clauses

### Install and collection

- **INSTALL-001 — One script tag with live install verification.** Settings show the snippet with a copy button. A **Verify installation** action loads the site's homepage server-side, finds the script and waits up to 60 seconds for a first event from that page. It then reports the exact cause of any failure: script missing, wrong site ID, origin mismatch, Content Security Policy blocking, consent flag never set, path excluded, or a blocker suspected. Until a first event arrives, the site shows **Awaiting first event** in the overview. **Acceptance evidence:** Point a fixture site at Vane without the script, then with the wrong site ID, then with a CSP that blocks it, then correctly. Each check names its cause; the correct one turns verified when the first event arrives.

- **INSTALL-002 — WordPress and WooCommerce plugin.** A WordPress.org plugin connects a WordPress site to a Vane installation with a site token. It inserts the script (or the proxied script from `INSTALL-006`), excludes logged-in administrators if the owner chooses, and turns on outbound-link, download, 404 and form events (`CONV-003`). With WooCommerce it records product views, add to cart, checkout and purchases with revenue (`CONV-007`). It shows a Vane dashboard widget in wp-admin. **Acceptance evidence:** Install the plugin on a WordPress + WooCommerce test site, connect it and place a test order. Pageviews, automatic events and a purchase with the correct USD revenue appear in Vane, and admin visits are excluded when that option is set.

- **INSTALL-003 — Shopify app with checkout tracking.** A Shopify app installs the script on the storefront through a theme app extension. It registers a Customer Events web pixel so that checkout steps and purchases (order value, currency, product lines) arrive as events with no customer personal data. Orders confirmed through the Shopify Admin API reconcile the purchases (`CONV-008`). **Acceptance evidence:** Install the app on a Shopify development store and complete a test checkout. Storefront pageviews, the checkout funnel steps and a reconciled purchase with its revenue appear.

- **INSTALL-004 — Webflow, Squarespace, Wix, Pane and static-site paths.** Step-by-step install guides with a verification check (`INSTALL-001`) cover Webflow (site-wide custom code), Squarespace (code injection), Wix (custom code or Velo) and common static hosts (Netlify, Vercel, GitHub Pages). Each guide also shows how to send the platform's form submissions as events. A [Pane](https://github.com/just-every/run-pane) site turns Vane on with one switch, and Pane's editor shows that page's visitors. **Acceptance evidence:** Follow each guide against its platform's test site and pass verification. Turn on Vane in a Pane site and see the page's visitors in the Pane editor.

- **INSTALL-005 — Google Tag Manager template.** A published GTM Community Template installs Vane, passes GTM consent state into `PRIVACY-003`, and maps dataLayer events to Vane custom events with declared properties. **Acceptance evidence:** Import the template into a GTM container, publish it and fire a dataLayer purchase. A Vane event with the mapped properties arrives, and denied consent sends nothing in a consent-required mode.

- **INSTALL-006 — First-party proxy so blockers do not erase data.** The owner can serve the script and collection endpoint from their own domain. Ready-made setups cover a Cloudflare Worker route (one click when the site's zone is in the same Cloudflare account), Next.js rewrites, Vercel, Netlify, Nginx, Apache and WordPress (`INSTALL-002`). A custom collection subdomain such as `stats.example.com` is also supported. The proxied path forwards the visitor's country and user agent for server-side derivation and never logs them. Reports label how much traffic arrived through the proxy (`COVERAGE-001`). **Acceptance evidence:** Configure the Cloudflare route and the Next.js rewrite, and load the site in Chromium with uBlock Origin's default lists active, including EasyPrivacy. First check that the unproxied `vane.js` is blocked. Events still arrive, the proxy share is reported, and the forwarded values are never stored raw.

- **INSTALL-007 — Single-page applications and hash routing.** The script records a pageview on every History API navigation and, when the owner turns it on, on hash changes. It also sends pageviews manually via `vane('pageview', {url, props})` for frameworks that control routing, and a manual or automatic pageview carries custom properties like any event (`CONV-002`). **Acceptance evidence:** Navigate a React Router and a hash-routed fixture through four views. Four pageviews arrive with the right paths and no duplicates, and a manual pageview with `props: {author: 'jo'}` breaks down by author.

- **INSTALL-008 — Server-side and mobile Events API.** An authenticated `POST /api/v1/events` accepts pageviews and custom events from servers, backends and mobile apps. It takes the same fields and properties as the script, plus a client IP and user agent supplied by the caller, which are used only for `METRIC-001` hashing and `DASH-005`/`DASH-006` derivation and then discarded. Tokens are scoped to one site, with rate limits and idempotency keys. **Acceptance evidence:** Send events from a server with a site token, with a duplicate idempotency key and with a token for another site. Events count once, the foreign token is refused, and no raw IP or user agent is stored.

- **COLLECT-002 — Open path collection with automatic redaction.** Every page on a registered site is collected without a path allowlist. Each event carries the page's hostname beside its path, and the hostname must be one of the site's allowed hostnames (`SITE-001`, `DASH-013`). The current allowlist becomes an optional include or exclude list. Query strings are dropped except UTM parameters, the `ref` and `source` parameters, and owner-allowlisted keys, such as a site-search parameter (`DASH-012`). As in Plausible, a `ref` or `source` value is kept by default as the visit's source when no `utm_source` is present, after the same redaction, so directory, Product Hunt and newsletter links keep their label. Ad click identifiers (gclid, fbclid, msclkid) are recorded only as a paid-click channel flag, never as values. Path segments that look like email addresses, long tokens, UUIDs or long numbers are replaced with placeholders before storage. Owners can add rewrite rules such as `/orders/*` → `/orders/:id`. Page titles are never stored. The exact rules: the include and exclude lists hold paths or patterns with the goal wildcards (`*` within a segment, `**` across), an empty include list means every path, and a page on an excluded path is refused. The hostname comes from the page's verified origin (`SITE-001`), lowercased and without its port. Each percent-decoded path segment is replaced, in this order, by `:email` when it contains `@`, `:uuid` when it is a UUID, `:number` when it holds six or more digits in a row, and `:token` when it is 16 or more letters, digits and `-_.=` holding a digit with no `-_.`-separated part that is a word of three or more letters (so slugs such as `top-10-brews-2024` stay); rewrite rules then apply in order and the first match wins. Each kept query value is trimmed and cut to 100 characters, and one that looks like an email address, a phone or account number or a web address is dropped. A visit's source is `utm_source`, else `ref` or `source`, else the referring hostname (a referrer on one of the site's own hostnames counts as direct), else direct. Imports apply the same path, exclusion and source rules; rows on excluded paths are dropped and counted in the import report. **Acceptance evidence:** Load pages with emails, tokens, order numbers and UTM and click-ID queries in the URL. Stored paths are redacted, UTM values and the paid-click flag are kept, and a rewrite rule groups the order pages. Visits landing on `/?ref=producthunt` and `/?source=newsletter` report `producthunt` and `newsletter` as their sources, and a visit with both `utm_source=google` and `ref=x` reports `google`.

- **COLLECT-003 — Bot, referrer-spam and internal-traffic exclusion.** At ingestion Vane drops known crawlers and automation, using the Cloudflare bot score where available plus a maintained user-agent list, data-centre traffic heuristics and a maintained referrer-spam list. Owners can exclude IP addresses or ranges (matched in memory and never stored), exclude pages, exclude up to 30 countries (ISO codes matched against Cloudflare geolocation at ingestion), and exclude their own browser. The own-browser opt-out is a link on the site's settings page that sets the `vane_ignore` flag declared in `COLLECT-001` in that browser only, and opening it again clears the flag. Excluded counts are reported by reason, never silently. **Acceptance evidence:** Send traffic from a headless browser, a spam referrer, an excluded IP, an excluded country and an opted-out browser beside real visits. Only the real visits count, and the exclusion report shows each reason and its count.

- **COLLECT-004 — Collection at small-business scale.** One installation collects at least 10 million pageviews a month across its sites with bursts of 500 events per second. Dashboards answer a 12-month query in under 2 seconds at that volume, using aggregated rollups and a declared analytical store (Cloudflare Analytics Engine, D1 rollups or the buyer's warehouse through `API-002`). Any limit is published and shown before it is reached: the current fixed 10,000-events-per-site-per-day admission ceiling is lifted. **Acceptance evidence:** A load test at the stated volume and burst rate admits every valid event exactly once. The 12-month dashboard query is timed, and the limit warning appears at the published threshold.

- **COVERAGE-001 — Coverage shown beside every number.** Every report states what it cannot see: the consent-declined share (the script sends a field-free declined counter when consent is not granted, if the owner enables it), sampling, exclusions by reason, the proxy and direct share, imported periods with unknown coverage, and deleted history. Where the site's Cloudflare zone is connected, Vane also compares its pageviews with zone page requests to estimate the share lost to blockers. No total is presented as complete when coverage is unknown. The exact rules: each share is its count over the page loads that reached Vane for it (that count plus the accepted page loads in the range); the declined-consent and exclusion counts hold only the site, day and reason; erased history is named with the day before which events were erased; and since blocked scripts are never measured until a zone is connected, every total is labelled partial. This is done when every coverage gap that exists today is shown: declined consent, privacy signals, excluded paths, sampling, imports with unknown coverage and erased history. Each later source of a gap shows its share before it ships: bot, referrer-spam, IP, country and own-browser exclusions (`COLLECT-003`), the proxy and direct share (`INSTALL-006`) and the Cloudflare zone comparison when a zone connection exists. The counts are site-wide under a Hostname filter (`DASH-013`). **Acceptance evidence:** Combine declined consent, sampling, exclusions, an import and a zone comparison on one site. The report shows each share with its denominator and labels the totals as partial.

### Visitors and engagement

- **METRIC-001 — Cookie-free unique visitors from daily-rotating salted hashes.** The server derives a visitor hash from SHA-256(daily salt + site ID + IP address + user agent). The salt is random, rotates at midnight in the site's time zone and is deleted after 24 hours. The IP and user agent are never stored, logged or returned. Unique visitors count distinct hashes within a day. For a multi-day range, the count is the sum of daily uniques unless consented mode (`METRIC-005`) applies, and the methodology page says so. A visitor cannot be followed across days or across sites. **Acceptance evidence:** Send pageviews from two simulated visitors over two days. Unique visitors are 2 per day and the range shows 4 under the stated method. Show that the database, logs and archive hold no IP, no user agent and no salt older than 24 hours, and that the same visitor hashes differently on another site.

- **METRIC-002 — Visits and pages per visit.** A visit is a run of events from one visitor hash with no gap over 30 minutes; it also ends when the daily salt rotates. Reports show visits, pageviews, pages per visit and views per visitor. **Acceptance evidence:** Send one visitor's pageviews with gaps of 10, 40 and 5 minutes. The report shows 2 visits, 4 pageviews and 2 pages per visit.

- **METRIC-003 — Bounce rate and visit duration.** A bounce is a visit with one pageview and no engagement event. Visit duration runs from the first event to the last engagement signal. The script sends a field-free engagement ping when the page is hidden or after 10 seconds or more of visible time, which counts time on the final page. Reports show the bounce rate and the median and average visit duration, per page as well as overall. **Acceptance evidence:** Simulate a quick bounce, a single-page visit with 45 seconds of engagement, and a three-page visit. The bounce rate, visit durations and per-page time match the definitions.

- **METRIC-004 — Methodology disclosed and versioned.** Every installation serves a public methodology page. It defines visitors, visits, bounce, duration, scroll depth, conversions and revenue, the hash recipe and salt lifetime, consented mode, the two declared browser-storage exceptions (`COLLECT-001`), how each metric combines in a multi-site roll-up (`DASH-010`), sampling and estimation limits. Each metric in the app links to its definition. A change to a definition is versioned and appears as an annotation on every chart it affects (`DASH-009`). **Acceptance evidence:** Open the methodology page signed out and follow each metric's definition link from the dashboard. The page states the roll-up rules and the two storage exceptions. Change the visit timeout and see a definition annotation dated on the charts.

- **METRIC-005 — Consented visitor mode: returning visitors and retention.** A site can turn on a consented mode for visitors who grant analytics consent. It stores a random first-party identifier with a lifetime the owner chooses (at most 13 months) and adds new and returning visitors, multi-day unique visitors, multi-visit attribution and weekly retention cohorts, as in Matomo. Visitors without consent stay in cookie-free mode, and reports show the consented share. Revoking consent deletes the identifier on the device and stops linking. **Acceptance evidence:** With consent granted, visit on day 1 and day 8 and see one returning visitor in the week-2 cohort. Without consent, the same visits count as cookie-free daily visitors. A revoke deletes the identifier.

- **METRIC-006 — Scroll depth and time on page.** The script reports the maximum scroll depth reached on each page as a whole percentage (0 to 100), as part of the engagement ping. Page reports show the average scroll depth, the share reaching 75%, and the time on page, and scroll-depth goals (`CONV-001`) count against the same figure. **Acceptance evidence:** Scroll a long fixture page to 50% and to 100% in separate visits. The page shows the average depth and the 75% share as expected.

### Dashboards and analysis

- **DASH-001 — Real-time dashboard.** A real-time view shows current visitors (active in the last 5 minutes), pageviews per minute for the last 30 minutes, and live top pages, sources and locations, refreshing at least every 15 seconds without a reload. **Acceptance evidence:** Send events while the real-time view is open. Current visitors and the live lists update within 15 seconds and drop once the visitors go idle for 5 minutes.

- **DASH-002 — Historical time series in the site's time zone.** The main chart plots visitors, visits, pageviews, bounce rate, duration, conversions and revenue by hour, day, week or month. Preset ranges cover today, yesterday, the last 7, 30 and 90 days, month to date, last month, year to date, the last 12 months and all time, and any custom range can be chosen. Days start at midnight in the site's time zone, which defaults to the owner's US time zone and handles daylight-saving changes. Every chart has an accessible data table. The exact rules: a range is local dates, end exclusive; the last 7, 30 and 90 days end with today, month and year to date run through today, the last 12 months start on the first of the month eleven months before this one, and all time starts on the site's earliest event day; filled From and Until dates make a custom range. The chart defaults to hourly up to 2 days, daily up to 92, weekly up to 731 and monthly beyond; hourly covers at most 31 days and daily at most two years. An hour is a real hour from local midnight, labelled with its local time and zone abbreviation, so a fall-back day has 25 hours (01:00 CDT, then 01:00 CST) and a spring-forward day 23; weeks start on Monday, and the first and last weeks and months are cut to the range. A bucket's visitors are its days' daily unique visitors summed (an hour counts its distinct visitor hashes); a visit, its bounce and its duration (the chart plots the average) belong to the bucket of its first page load; pageviews and goal conversions count where they happened. Imported history has dates but no times, so it appears in daily, weekly and monthly buckets and not hourly ones. The add-site form fills in the time zone the owner's browser reports; a site prepared without one, such as through the command API, uses UTC until the owner changes it. This is done when every metric that exists today is charted; revenue joins the chart and its data table when revenue goals ship (`CONV-006`), and `CONV-006` is not done until it does. **Acceptance evidence:** Seed events either side of a US daylight-saving change and midnight in America/Chicago. The daily buckets follow local days, each preset range returns the expected totals, and the data table matches the chart.

- **DASH-003 — Page, entry and exit reports.** Page reports list top pages, entry (landing) pages and exit pages, each with visitors, pageviews, bounce rate, time on page, scroll depth and conversion rate. Pages can be grouped by path prefix or by a rewrite rule. When a site has more than one hostname, each row shows its hostname and the same path on two hostnames is two rows (`DASH-013`). **Acceptance evidence:** Seed visits with known entry and exit pages. The three tables match, and grouping by `/blog/*` sums the posts correctly.

- **DASH-004 — Channels, referrers and UTM campaigns.** Every visit gets a channel, using published definitions: Direct, Organic Search, Paid Search, Organic Social, Paid Social, Email, Referral, AI Assistants (ChatGPT, Perplexity, Claude, Gemini, Copilot), Affiliates and Display. Referrer domains and URLs come from public referring pages. All five UTM fields (source, medium, campaign, term, content) are available as dimensions. No allowlist is needed. Email-client and webmail referrers are grouped. Every channel, referrer and UTM table shows, per row, the visitors, visits, bounce rate, visit duration and the conversion rate for the selected goal, as the pages table does. The exact rules: the channel is set at collection by the first matching rule, in the order Affiliates, Display, Paid Social, Paid Search, AI Assistants, Email, Organic Search, Organic Social, Referral, Direct, from the visit's source (`COLLECT-002`), `utm_medium` and paid-click flag, with the source lists and the paid `utm_medium` values published on the methodology page (`METRIC-004`). A referring page is kept as its hostname and path, redacted like page paths, without query or fragment; email-client and webmail referrers are grouped as the source `webmail` and their addresses are never kept. Each visit counts under its first page load's channel, source, referring page and UTM values. The conversion rate counts visits with the goal the reader selects, or with any goal when none is selected; the page tables follow the same selection. Imported history has no visits, so it is left out of these tables. **Acceptance evidence:** Send visits with Google organic, Google paid (gclid flag), Facebook, an email UTM, a chatgpt.com referrer and five-field UTMs. Each lands in its documented channel, every UTM field is reportable, and each row's visitors, bounce rate and visit duration equal a hand-computed count of the seeded visits.

- **DASH-005 — Country, region and city.** Location comes from Cloudflare's request geolocation at ingestion: country, region (US state), city and US metro area (DMA), stored as names and never as an IP address. On shared links, embeds and public dashboards (`SHARE-001`, `SHARE-002`), a city with fewer than 5 visitors in the selected range is folded into an "Other cities" row in its region. Signed-in team members see every city. **Acceptance evidence:** Send events with Cloudflare geo for Texas, Ontario and London. Reports show the country, region and city, and no IP appears anywhere in storage. Seed Austin with 12 visitors and Marfa with 3, then open a shared link. Austin is listed, Marfa is folded into "Other cities" in Texas, and the signed-in dashboard lists both.

- **DASH-006 — Devices, browsers and operating systems.** The server derives the device class (desktop, mobile, tablet), browser and major version, operating system and version, and a screen-size class from the user agent and an optional viewport width. The raw user agent is discarded. **Acceptance evidence:** Send events from iPhone Safari, Android Chrome, Windows Edge and an iPad. The device, browser and OS tables match, and no raw user agent is stored.

- **DASH-007 — Filters, segments and saved views.** Clicking any dimension value filters the whole dashboard. - **DASH-008 — Period comparisons.** Any range can be compared with the previous period, the same period last year or a custom period. - **DASH-009 — Annotations, manual and automatic.** Team members add dated notes to charts. - **DASH-010 — Multi-site overview and roll-up.** The sites list shows each site's visitors, change and a sparkline for the selected range, and the list can be sorted and searched. An owner can create a roll-up view that combines several sites, for example the marketing site and the store. Every report, filter and breakdown works on the roll-up, and each row can be split by site. The roll-up never de-duplicates people across sites, because `METRIC-001` hashes the site ID into every visitor hash. So each metric combines by a stated rule. Visitors and visits are the sum of each site's own counts, labelled "summed per site", with a note that one person on two sites counts once per site. Pageviews, events, conversions and revenue are exact sums. Bounce rate, visit duration and conversion rate are recomputed from the summed visits and conversions, not averaged across sites. The methodology page (`METRIC-004`) states these rules, and the roll-up links to them. A site with consented mode (`METRIC-005`) still counts its visitors on its own, so the roll-up never links one consented identifier across sites. **Acceptance evidence:** With three sites, open the overview, sort it by change and create a roll-up of two of them. Send one simulated visitor (same IP and user agent) to both sites on one day, and two other visitors to one site each. The roll-up shows 4 visitors and 4 visits, labelled "summed per site", with the cross-site note. Its pageviews, conversions and revenue equal the exact sums. Its bounce rate equals the summed bounced visits over the summed visits. The site split gives each site's own figures, and the methodology page states each rule.

- **DASH-011 — Journeys and paths.** From any page or entry page, a journey view shows the next and previous three steps within visits, with counts and drop-off, and it can be filtered by segment. **Acceptance evidence:** Seed visits through home → pricing → signup and home → blog → exit. - **DASH-012 — Site-search analytics.** When the owner names the site-search query parameter, Vane reports search terms, searches per visit, the pages searches start from and the share of visits that exit after searching. - **DASH-013 — Hostnames and subdomains within one site.** A site that accepts several hostnames (`SITE-001`), for example `www.example.com`, `blog.example.com` and `app.example.com`, records the hostname of every pageview and event (`COLLECT-002`) as a dimension. The hostname is stored lowercased and without its port, and only when it is one of the site's allowed hostnames. A Hostnames report shows visitors, visits, pageviews, bounce rate and conversions per hostname. Page, entry and exit tables keep the same path on different hostnames apart (`DASH-003`). A Hostname filter (is, is not, contains, and matches with wildcards such as `*.example.com`) narrows every report, funnel, goal, alert, shared link, export and Stats API answer (`DASH-007`, `API-001`), and it can be saved in a segment. A goal or funnel step can be scoped to a hostname. The exact rules: the filter compares lowercased values, `matches` takes `*` wildcards only, and rows without a hostname (imported history and page loads collected before hostnames were stored) never match a hostname filter; the field-free privacy-signal count (`PRIVACY-002`) carries no hostname and stays site-wide. A goal is scoped by prefixing its hostname, as in `blog.example.com/thanks`. The Hostnames report counts visitors, visits, pageviews, bounce rate and conversions (visits with a goal, or with the selected goal) per hostname. This is done when every report and door that exists honours the filter: the dashboard, its page, source, channel, UTM, device, search, goal and automatic-event tables, and the JSON summary that `API-001` versions. Each later door inherits the obligation before it ships: segments and saved views (`DASH-007`), funnels and funnel-step scoping (`CONV-005`), alerts (`ALERT-001`, `ALERT-002`), shared links (`SHARE-001`), CSV export (`API-002`), scheduled reports (`REPORT-001`) and the versioned Stats API (`API-001`). **Acceptance evidence:** On one site with `www.` and `blog.` hostnames, send `/` and `/pricing` from each. The pages table shows four rows, each with its hostname. The Hostname filter `is blog.example.com` narrows the dashboard, the Stats API answer and a CSV export to the blog's events. `is not` and `contains` return the complements and matches, a wildcard matches both hostnames, and a segment saved with the filter gives the same totals in a scheduled report. An event from a hostname that is not allowed is refused.

- **DASH-014 — Named sources for referrer domains and app referrers.** Every referrer is reported under a named source from a maintained source list that Vane publishes with its methodology (`METRIC-004`). Web and app referrers for the same service group together: `news.ycombinator.com` reports as Hacker News, `android-app://com.slack.android`, `slack.com` and `app.slack.com` as Slack, `l.facebook.com`, `m.facebook.com` and `android-app://com.facebook.katana` as Facebook, and `t.co` as X. The source feeds the channel definitions (`DASH-004`), and the referrer domains and app identifiers stay available as a drill-down beneath each source. A referrer that is not on the list reports under its own domain, and an owner can add grouping rules of their own through `APPROVE-001`. The exact rules: a list entry is a hostname (covering its subdomains, so `l.facebook.com` is Facebook), a hostname prefix ending in a dot (any top-level domain, with or without `www.`) or an `android-app://` app ID, and the first match wins; the site's approved grouping rules are tried before the list and regroup history already collected. Names a site sends itself as `utm_source`, `ref` or `source` are reported as sent (`COLLECT-002`); only referrer domains and app IDs are named. Each named source's kind (search engine, social network, AI assistant, email) feeds the channel definitions, and its drill-down lists the lowercased referrer domains and app IDs of the visits it names. **Acceptance evidence:** Send visits with the referrers `https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=1`, `android-app://com.slack.android`, `https://app.slack.com/` and `https://l.facebook.com/`, and an unlisted `https://brewblog.example/`. The sources table shows Hacker News, Slack with two visits, Facebook and `brewblog.example`, and Slack's drill-down lists both of its referrers. An approved owner rule naming `brewblog.example` as Brew Blog regroups that row, and the published source list names every grouping used.

- **PERF-001 — Page performance.** The script can report Core Web Vitals (LCP, INP, CLS) and page load time, without identifiers, and Vane shows them per page and device class at the 75th percentile. - **HEAT-001 — Aggregate click and scroll maps.** For pages the owner chooses, Vane records click positions on a grid and scroll depth, without identifiers, form contents or text. ### Conversions and revenue

- **CONV-001 — Pageview goals with patterns and conversion rates.** A goal can be a single path or a wildcard pattern with Plausible's meaning: `*` matches within one path segment and `**` across segments (`/thank-you*`, `/thanks/*`, `/blog/**`). Reports show conversions, unique converting visitors and the conversion rate against visitors. A goal can also be a scroll-depth goal: visitors reaching N% (any whole percentage) of a page or pattern, such as 75% on `/blog/**`, counted from `METRIC-006`, with the same uniques and conversion rate. Plausible's scroll-depth goals import as these (`IMPORT-008`). **Acceptance evidence:** Define `/thanks/*` and `/thanks**` and visit `/thanks/pro` and `/thanks/pro/annual`. `/thanks/*` converts on `/thanks/pro` only (`*` stays within one path segment, as in Plausible), `/thanks**` converts on both, and the conversion rate equals converting visitors divided by visitors. Define a 75% scroll goal on `/blog/**`. A visitor scrolling a post to 80% converts and one scrolling to 50% does not.

- **CONV-002 — Custom events with properties.** Sites send events through `vane('Signup', {props: {plan: 'pro'}})` or by adding CSS classes (`vane-event-name=Signup`, with property classes). An event can carry up to 30 properties with string or number values. Property keys and values pass the `COLLECT-002` redaction, and an event carrying an email address is refused. Properties are dimensions for filtering, breakdowns and funnels. Pageviews carry properties the same way, for example author or category on a blog post (`INSTALL-007`). The script also accepts the Plausible and Fathom call and class syntax, so a switched site keeps counting before any code changes (`IMPORT-008`). **Acceptance evidence:** Fire Signup with plan=pro and plan=free through JavaScript and CSS classes. The breakdown by plan matches, and a property holding an email address is refused. A pageview with author=jo breaks down by author.

- **CONV-003 — Automatic outbound-link, file-download, 404 and form-submission events.** Each automatic event is turned on per site. Outbound links record the destination URL without its query, downloads record the file path for common document and media extensions, 404 pages record the missing path (the site marks its 404 page), and form submissions record the form's ID or action path, never field values. **Acceptance evidence:** Click an outbound link and a PDF link, open a 404 page and submit a form on a fixture site. Four events arrive with the right properties and no form contents.

- **CONV-004 — Conversion rates by source, campaign, landing page and audience.** Every breakdown (channel, referrer, UTM, entry page, country, device) can show the conversions, conversion rate and revenue for a chosen goal. **Acceptance evidence:** Seed conversions from Google and from email. The channel table shows each channel's conversion rate and revenue for the selected goal.

- **CONV-005 — Funnels.** An owner or analyst defines a funnel of 2 to 8 steps (pageviews, wildcard paths or custom events with property conditions), ordered within one visit, or within one visitor across visits in consented mode. The funnel report shows the entrants, conversion and drop-off at each step, the median time between steps and a segment filter. Clicking a drop-off opens the journeys (`DASH-011`). **Acceptance evidence:** Define the funnel product → cart → checkout → purchase and seed visits that drop at each step. The step counts and rates match, and the segment filter recomputes them.

- **CONV-006 — Revenue goals.** A custom event can carry a revenue amount and an ISO currency. USD is the default reporting currency, and other currencies are converted at the day's stored exchange rate, which is shown. Reports show total revenue, average order value, and revenue per visitor by source, campaign, page and segment. Payment-processor referrers (Stripe Checkout, PayPal, Shop Pay, Klarna, Afterpay) are excluded from attribution, so the original source keeps credit. **Acceptance evidence:** Record USD and CAD purchases with a Stripe Checkout return referrer. Revenue totals in USD with the stated rate, and attribution goes to the original source rather than Stripe.

- **CONV-007 — Ecommerce events.** Standard events cover product view, add to cart, begin checkout and purchase, with a product SKU, name, category, quantity and price. WooCommerce (`INSTALL-002`), Shopify (`INSTALL-003`) and [Stall](https://github.com/just-every/run-stall) emit them automatically, and a ready-made ecommerce funnel and a product-performance report come with them. **Acceptance evidence:** Run a test purchase through each of the three platforms. The ecommerce funnel and the product report show the product, quantity and revenue.

- **CONV-008 — Conversions reconciled with real orders and leads.** Connected outcome sources confirm browser conversions by an opaque order or submission ID, never by customer details. Stripe confirms through webhooks, Shopify and WooCommerce through their order APIs, and Stall through its events. Lead and booking sources are HubSpot forms and CRM, [Orbit](https://github.com/just-every/run-orbit), [Pencil](https://github.com/just-every/run-pencil) and [Dial](https://github.com/just-every/run-dial). Reports show browser-reported, confirmed, unconfirmed and server-only outcomes side by side, with refunds subtracted, and attribute a server-only outcome to a source only when its browser event matched. **Acceptance evidence:** Complete three Stripe test payments, one with the browser event blocked and one refunded, and two HubSpot form submissions, one later marked qualified. The report shows each outcome in its correct confirmed or unconfirmed column, with net revenue.

- **CONV-009 — No-code event definitions.** In the app, an owner or analyst can define events without editing the site: a click on a CSS selector, a form submission, an element becoming visible, or a pageview pattern. A definition is previewed against the live page and applied through `APPROVE-001`, and the script picks it up from the site configuration. **Acceptance evidence:** Define a click event for `#book-demo`, preview the matched element and approve it. Clicks on the live fixture arrive as the event, with no site code change.

- **EXPERIMENT-001 — A/B tests with honest results.** An owner sets up a split test of 2 to 4 variants on a page. Assignment is stable within a visitor's day in cookie-free mode, and across days in consented mode. The site applies a variant through the script API, or through Pane or the buyer's code. Results show each variant's visitors, conversions and conversion rate with a 95% interval, the probability that it beats control, a minimum-sample warning, and a sample-ratio-mismatch check. A test cannot be called while it is underpowered. **Acceptance evidence:** Run a fixture test with a seeded 20% lift and a null test. The first reaches significance with the expected interval, the null test never does, and a skewed split triggers the mismatch warning.

- **IMPORT-007 — Imported history merges without double counting.** Each import declares a cut-over date. For overlapping days, live data wins and the overlap is reported. - **IMPORT-008 — Tracking cut-over keeps incumbent events counting.** On the day the buyer swaps the incumbent's script for Vane's, their custom events, conversions and revenue keep arriving with no page edit, and their goal definitions come with them. The cut-over has three parts.
  - *Compatible calls.* With the owner's per-site *Incumbent compatibility* setting on, `vane.js` defines `window.plausible` and `window.fathom` shims. `plausible(name, {props, revenue: {amount, currency}, interactive, callback})` and its pre-load queue (`window.plausible.q`) become Vane custom events with properties and revenue. The `plausible-event-name=` CSS classes become events too, in both the `=` and the `--` form, with `plausible-event-<prop>=` property classes and `+` read as a space. `fathom.trackEvent(name, {_value})` becomes an event whose `_value` is read in cents and converted to the site currency (USD by default, `CONV-006`), and `fathom.trackPageview()` becomes a pageview. The shim also reads the options a Plausible snippet configures, so pageview properties and automatic events do not stop silently after cut-over. These are the `plausible.init({...})` options `customProperties` (pageview properties, as an object or a function), `fileDownloads` with its `fileExtensions` list, `outboundLinks`, `formSubmissions`, `hashBasedRouting`, `autoCapturePageviews` and `captureOnLocalhost`, plus the legacy script's file-name extensions (such as `script.hash.outbound-links.file-downloads.js`) and its `event-<prop>` attributes. Each maps to the matching Vane setting for that page (`INSTALL-007`, `CONV-002`, `CONV-003`). An option Vane does not support is listed in the call-site scan, never dropped silently. Every shimmed call passes the same `COLLECT-001`/`COLLECT-002` field rules and redaction as a native call, so an incumbent call carrying an email address is refused. The incumbent's own script tag, if left in place, is detected and reported.
  - *Call-site scan.* Install verification (`INSTALL-001`) crawls the site's sitemap, up to the owner's page limit, and lists every page that still loads an incumbent script or fires an incumbent call or class. For each one it drafts the native `vane()` rewrite, so the shim can be retired. The owner can hand the drafts to `AGENT-001` to open as a pull request or a Pane change.
  - *Definitions carried over.* Vane proposes the incumbent's pageview, custom-event, scroll-depth and revenue goals and its custom-property names under the same names, so imported history and live conversions line up. Each definition comes from the source the buyer's incumbent plan actually exposes, and funnels are rebuilt through the guided step, as `IMPORT-009` sets out. ### Reports, alerts and follow-up

- **REPORT-001 — Scheduled email reports.** Each team member, or an external recipient the owner adds, can subscribe to daily, weekly or monthly email reports for chosen sites and segments. Reports are sent at a chosen local time in the site's time zone. Each one shows the headline metrics with their comparison, top pages, sources, goals and revenue, the coverage notes and the explanation (`REPORT-003`). Every email has a one-click unsubscribe. A temporary delivery failure is retried 3 times, after 15 minutes, 2 hours and 12 hours, using the same message ID so the report is never sent twice. A permanent bounce is not retried. Either way the failure shows in the app with its reason. A weekly report goes out on the weekday the recipient chooses (Monday by default), and a monthly report on the first day of the month. **Acceptance evidence:** Subscribe two recipients to the weekly report at 8:00 America/New_York and run the schedule across a daylight-saving change. Each email arrives once at the local hour with figures that match the dashboard, and unsubscribe stops it. With a fake mail provider and clock, fail one delivery temporarily twice: it is retried at 15 minutes and 2 hours and delivered once. Fail another on all four attempts: it shows as failed after the 12-hour retry. A permanent bounce shows in the app at once and is not retried. A weekly report set to Mondays at 8:00 am arrives on Monday at 8:00 local time and on no other day.

- **REPORT-002 — Slack, Microsoft Teams and Nook delivery.** Reports and alerts can be sent to a Slack channel (Slack app with OAuth), a Microsoft Teams channel (Teams app or workflow webhook) or a [Nook](https://github.com/just-every/run-nook) channel, with a link back to the filtered dashboard. **Acceptance evidence:** Against fixture endpoints for all three, send a weekly report and an alert. Each payload carries the right figures and a working deep link, and a revoked token surfaces as a delivery failure.

- **REPORT-003 — A readable explanation of what changed.** Every report and alert explains its change by its largest contributors: which channels, pages, campaigns, locations or devices moved, and by how much, with denominators. It includes annotations in the window and any coverage change. It labels a change "within normal variation" exactly when the value falls inside the 95% prediction interval of `ALERT-001`'s day-of-week baseline, and it shows that interval. The explanation never claims a cause. **Acceptance evidence:** Seed a week where organic search falls 40% on one page while other channels are stable. The explanation names that page and channel with its denominators, lists the overlapping annotation, and labels a fluctuation inside the baseline's 95% prediction interval as normal variation and one outside it as not.

- **ALERT-001 — Traffic spike and drop alerts.** An owner sets an alert on visitors, visits or pageviews for a site or segment. It fires on a fixed threshold, or on an anomaly: a value outside the 95% prediction interval of a day-of-week and hour baseline that accounts for US federal holidays and the owner's business calendar. Alerts have quiet hours, a cool-down and a recipients list across email, Slack, Teams, Nook and webhook. A spike alert can also watch current visitors (`DASH-001`) against a fixed threshold, as Plausible's traffic-spike notification does. Spike and current-visitor conditions are evaluated at least every 5 minutes, and the alert is sent within 15 minutes of the threshold being crossed. The owner can enter scheduled maintenance windows, one-off or recurring, and an Uptime maintenance window arrives the same way. A drop inside a window is held, not sent, and the held alert is recorded in the alert log and on the chart with its reason. **Acceptance evidence:** Seed eight weeks of baseline traffic, then inject a 4× spike, a 70% drop and a Thanksgiving-day dip. The spike and drop alert once each, the holiday dip does not, and the cool-down suppresses a repeat. With a current-visitors threshold of 50 and a fake clock, raise current visitors above 50. The alert is sent within 15 minutes of the crossing, and the recorded crossing and send times show it. Enter a recurring Saturday 2:00–3:00 am maintenance window and inject a 90% drop inside it. No alert is sent, and the alert log shows the drop as held with the reason scheduled maintenance; the same drop at 4:00 am alerts.

- **ALERT-002 — Conversion, revenue and tracking-health alerts.** Alerts also cover a goal or revenue drop and a conversion-rate change. Tracking-health alerts cover a site with prior traffic that goes silent, a goal that stops firing after a deploy, a surge in collection errors, a collapse in the consent rate, and a divergence between browser-reported and confirmed outcomes (`CONV-008`). Tracking health also covers a page or template that stops sending pageviews while the rest of the site still does. The site overview shows a tracking-health line: how many goals are tracking out of how many, and when the check last ran. The check runs at least daily, at a local time the owner sets (9:00 am by default). **Acceptance evidence:** Remove the script from a fixture site, break a goal's event name and block browser purchase events while Stripe confirms orders. Each condition raises its own named alert. Drop the script from `/pricing` only while the other pages keep sending, and a page-silent alert names `/pricing`. With twelve goals that all fire, the overview reads all 12 goals tracking with the time of the last check; break one, and the next check reads 11 of 12 and names the silent goal.

- **FOLLOW-001 — Alerts are followed through until a person resolves them.** Each alert opens an item with a responsible person, a next due action and a due time in local business hours. The item can be acknowledged, assigned, commented on and resolved with an outcome: real change, tracking fault fixed, expected, or ignored. Unacknowledged items remind the responsible person, then escalate to a second recipient, on the owner's schedule. Reminders stop when the item is resolved, the metric recovers and a person confirms it, or the owner ends the policy. A delivery or connector failure keeps the item open, and an alert notification alone never closes it. **Acceptance evidence:** Fire a drop alert, leave it unacknowledged past the reminder time, then the escalation time. ## Push further with agents

These agents work on the site's own analytics, its connected outcome sources and the grants the owner gives. They go beyond chat over charts: they keep the measurement trustworthy, act within granted authority and measure what happened. Every agent action is audited (`AUDIT-001`), and every change an agent ships is annotated (`DASH-009`) and can be reversed. No agent may add an identifier, bypass consent, change a metric definition silently, invent revenue, or match identities across sites.

- **AGENT-001 — Maintain trustworthy instrumentation.** The agent compares the declared goals, events and funnels with what actually arrives. It also reads the site's pages (by crawling the sitemap), deploy diffs from a connected GitHub repository or Pane, the consent state, and server-side counts from Stripe, Shopify or the CRM (`CONV-008`). It finds missing, duplicated, misnamed and newly broken events, pages without the script, and funnels whose steps no longer exist. For each problem it drafts a fix: a pull request to the site repository, a GTM template change, a Pane setting or a no-code event (`CONV-009`). After the fix ships it verifies the real payload. A human approves each fix, and the agent never widens the collected fields. **Acceptance evidence:** Deploy a fixture site change that renames the signup button and drops the script from one template. The agent reports both with evidence and drafts a PR and a no-code event. After the owner approves and the change deploys, the agent confirms the events arrive again and annotates the fix.

- **AGENT-002 — Explain why traffic or conversions moved.** When numbers change, the agent separates four causes. Tracking coverage covers event volume against confirmed outcomes, consent rate, script errors and blockers. Channel mix covers the contributions by source, campaign and search query (`SEARCH-001`). Page behaviour covers funnel steps, page speed (`PERF-001`) and errors from Spool. External events cover Uptime incidents, Mango sends, ad-spend changes, seasonality and US holidays. It returns ranked hypotheses, each with denominators, linked evidence, its confidence and the observation that would disprove it. Missing coverage is stated as missing, never as a cause, and the agent never claims causality from a correlation. **Acceptance evidence:** Seed three fixture weeks: a tracking break, an organic ranking drop, and a real checkout failure that confirmed orders corroborate. For each, the agent ranks the true cause first with cited evidence and names what is unknown.

- **AGENT-003 — Propose and ship a site or campaign change under granted authority.** From an explanation or an opportunity (`AGENT-006`), the agent drafts a concrete change. The change can be page copy, a call to action, a title and meta description, a broken link or a form fix, a UTM correction, or a campaign budget or schedule change. It states the expected effect, its guardrail metrics and how it will be measured (`AGENT-004`). The agent ships only through grants the owner has given: a Pane page edit, a pull request to the site's GitHub repository, a Webflow CMS item, a Mango campaign draft, or an ad-platform change. Spend changes and material copy always need a person's approval, and every grant can be revoked. A 404 path that gets visits (`CONV-003`), such as a mistyped `/pricng`, gets a drafted redirect to the page it most likely meant. **Acceptance evidence:** Grant Pane page-edit and GitHub PR access but no ad access. The agent ships an approved CTA change in Pane and opens a PR for a title change. Its proposed budget change waits for approval and cannot be shipped without the ad grant, and revoking the Pane grant blocks the next edit. From a 404 report where `/pricng` has 37 visits, the agent drafts a redirect to `/pricing` and opens it as a PR under the GitHub grant. Once it is merged and deployed, the next visit to `/pricng` lands on `/pricing`, and the path's 404 count stops growing.

- **AGENT-004 — Measure the result against coverage gaps.** For every shipped change, the agent runs an experiment (`EXPERIMENT-001`) where it can, or a before-and-after comparison with matched weekdays and guardrails where it cannot. It reports the measured effect with an interval, whether coverage changed in the window, and a verdict: measured improvement, no detectable change, worse, or inconclusive because of coverage or sample size. It recommends keeping or reverting the change, and it reverts only under the grant, with approval. **Acceptance evidence:** Ship two fixture changes: one with a real lift under an experiment, and one before-and-after change during a consent-rate drop. The first is reported as a measured improvement with its interval, the second as inconclusive because of coverage, and an approved revert restores the prior page.

- **AGENT-005 — Pursue anomalies to resolution.** On an alert (`ALERT-001`, `ALERT-002`), the agent investigates with `AGENT-002` first and attaches its findings to the follow-up item (`FOLLOW-001`). It contacts the responsible person only when a decision or an outside fix is needed, for example a developer for a broken event or the marketer for a paused campaign, on the owner's channels and schedule. It keeps the item open until it is resolved, and it closes it only on a person's confirmation or a verified recovery with coverage intact. **Acceptance evidence:** Fire a silent-site alert caused by a deploy. The agent attaches its evidence, messages the configured developer once, reminds them after the due time, and after the fix verifies events and closes the item with that evidence. A real traffic dip with no fault is closed as expected after the owner confirms.

- **AGENT-006 — Weekly growth brief with opportunities.** Each week the agent writes a brief of three to five opportunities, each with figures. Examples are pages with high entry and high exit, campaigns that convert above the site's average, Search Console queries ranking 4 to 20 with high impressions, slow pages that convert below their peers, and stalled funnel steps. It links each one to a drafted `AGENT-003` change. The owner can dismiss an opportunity type, and the agent stops raising it. **Acceptance evidence:** On a fixture site with seeded opportunities, the brief names each with correct figures and a linked draft. A dismissed type does not return the next week.

- **AGENT-007 — Answer questions in plain English, with the query shown.** In the app, Slack, Teams and MCP, a team member asks a question such as "Which campaigns brought paying customers from Texas last month?". The answer comes back as a chart, the figures, and the exact filters, metrics and range used. The figures equal what the dashboard shows for that query. Ambiguous questions get a clarifying choice, and questions the data cannot answer (people, causes, data outside coverage) say so. **Acceptance evidence:** Ask ten fixture questions, including an ambiguous one and an unanswerable one. Every numeric answer equals the Stats API result for the shown query, the ambiguous question gets a choice and the unanswerable one says why.

## Honest boundaries

- **No identity graph, fingerprinting or ad-exchange attribution.** Vane measures first-party traffic under declared modes. It never builds cross-site identities, fingerprints or ad audiences, and it does not place ad pixels. Buyers who need platform conversion tracking keep the ad platform's own tag, through GTM. Vane reads campaign spend under `ADS-001` and changes campaigns only under an `AGENT-003` grant.
- **"Cookieless" is not a legal guarantee.** Vane shows its exact fields, identifier lifetimes and consent behaviour (`PRIVACY-001`) and supports consent managers (`PRIVACY-003`). The owner and their counsel choose the mode. Vane does not certify compliance with GDPR, ePrivacy, CCPA/CPRA or any other state law.
- **Counts are estimates with stated coverage.** Visitors are hashed daily estimates, not people. Revenue and leads are confirmed only through the connected Stripe, Shopify, WooCommerce, Stall, CRM and form sources (`CONV-008`), and causal lift is claimed only under an experiment (`EXPERIMENT-001`). Blocked and declined traffic is estimated and disclosed, never filled in.
- **No individual session replay or per-visitor log.** Vane offers aggregate heatmaps, scroll maps, journeys and funnels. It has no per-visit record of one person's actions, such as Matomo's Visits Log and Visitor Profile, because it keeps no per-person history. Journeys (`DASH-011`) answer the path questions in aggregate. A buyer who needs per-visitor recordings uses PostHog, Microsoft Clarity or Hotjar under their own consent; Vane links to it from annotations and does not store recordings.
- **No logged-in product analytics with person profiles.** Website and app traffic through the Events API is in scope. Per-user product analytics, feature flags and person profiles stay in PostHog, Amplitude or Mixpanel. Vane's webhooks and warehouse export (`API-002`, `API-003`) feed them.
- **Tag management and SEO tooling are not rebuilt.** Third-party marketing tags stay in Google Tag Manager, which Vane installs through (`INSTALL-005`). Backlink, rank-tracking and site-audit suites (Ahrefs, Semrush) stay separate. Vane reads Search Console and Bing keyword data.
- **Neighbouring jobs stay with their products.** Website building is Pane, storefronts are Stall or Shopify, CRM is Orbit or HubSpot, forms are Pencil, bookings are Dial, email campaigns are Mango, uptime is Uptime, errors are Spool, feedback is Penny and automation is Crank. Vane integrates with each of them.
- **Raw event storage has a capacity design.** History is stored as the rollups and anonymous events declared in `COLLECT-004` within published limits. Unlimited raw history goes to the buyer's warehouse (`API-002`).
- **US first.** USD reporting, US time zones and daylight saving, US federal holidays in baselines, US state privacy signals and a US data location are the defaults. Other currencies, regions and the EU jurisdiction are supported, but not first.

## Declared data flows

Today Vane receives only page-load events from registered origins, sends only sign-in and invitation mail through the configured Base mail adapter, and declares Cloudflare as its only external in `seed.json`. Verify installation (`INSTALL-001`) sends one GET for the site's homepage at its registered origin, following up to five redirects. It carries no visitor data, keeps nothing beyond the pass or fail result it reports to the owner, and needs no `seed.json` externals entry because it reads the owner's own public page. Each target integration is a new outbound data flow. These are Google Search Console, Google Analytics, Bing, ad platforms, Stripe, Shopify, WooCommerce, HubSpot, Slack, Microsoft Teams, warehouses, consent managers, Pane, Stall, Orbit, Pencil, Dial, Mango, Uptime, Spool, Nook, Crank, GitHub, Webflow and MCP clients. Each one ships only with its `seed.json` externals entry, its scopes, the data it sends and receives, and token custody (encrypted at rest, revocable and excluded from archives) declared in this section.
